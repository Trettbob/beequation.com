BRF files are for braille embossers and braille displays. Unicode braille text has the same cells and page layout. Tactile picture PDFs are for swell paper; shaded parts use hatching.

Computer-made braille, not yet proofread. Please check with a braille reader.

Take the files to your child’s vision teacher or school. Ask them to check the braille and adapt the pictures before use. The EPUB contains text descriptions and answers for libraries and braille transcription services.

For swell paper (microcapsule) or a tactile embosser. Print at 100%.

Column sums are written as linear calculations, for example 345 + 278 =.

English uses uncontracted Unified English Braille (UEB), including UEB maths. Other languages use their local uncontracted tables.

Free to share and adapt under CC BY 4.0, with credit to Beequation (beequation.com).

Where to ask

In the UK, ask your council’s sensory or vision support service or your child’s qualified teacher of children and young people with vision impairment (QTVI). These services are free to families.

  • RNIB Bookshare: free for eligible UK schools and services.
  • ClearVision: free postal loans of tactile books to families in the UK and Ireland.
  • Living Paintings: a free tactile book library.

In the USA

  • APH Federal Quota: ask your school about free materials through this programme.
  • Bookshare: free for qualifying US students.
  • National Braille Press ReadBooks!: free braille materials for children aged 0–7.

Libraries and services may transcribe, adapt and share these worksheets under the same licence.
