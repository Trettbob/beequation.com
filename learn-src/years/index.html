In England, children are grouped into school years by age. The school year runs from September to July, and everyone born between 1 September and the following 31 August is in the same year group. Children start in Reception in the September after they turn 4, then move up a year each September until Year 6, the last year of primary school.

If you're in the US, the grade is roughly one lower than the UK year number: Year 3 is about the same as Grade 2. Each guide below follows the National Curriculum in England (and the EYFS framework for Reception), says what children learn that year, and gives ideas, a challenge and free worksheets to help at home.

Choose a year group

Age 4 to 5. Counting, subitising, numbers to 10 and number bonds to 5, all through play. Age 5 to 6. Counting to 100, number bonds to 20, halves and quarters, and o'clock. Age 6 to 7. Tens and ones, the 2, 5 and 10 times tables, money, change and time. Age 7 to 8. Numbers to 1,000, column methods and the 3, 4 and 8 times tables. Age 8 to 9. All times tables to 12 × 12, decimals and the Multiplication Tables Check. Age 9 to 10. Long multiplication, prime numbers, fractions, decimals and percentages. Age 10 to 11. Ratio, algebra, long division and the Key Stage 2 SATs.

The ages are the age at the start and end of the school year: a child in Year 3 is 7 in September and turns 8 at some point before the following August. Scotland and Northern Ireland number their primary years differently (Primary 1 to Primary 7) and, like Wales, have their own curriculum, so the details there are a little different.

Don't worry if your child seems ahead in one topic and behind in another. Children learn at different speeds, and summer-born children can be almost a year younger than others in their class. Use the guides to see what's coming next, and if something is worrying you, a quick chat with the class teacher usually helps more than extra worksheets.
