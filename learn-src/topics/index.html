Every topic in primary maths, explained for the grown-ups who help at home or in class. Each guide sets out what children learn in each year group, the big ideas with pictures and real numbers, the mistakes to look out for, quick games to play and free worksheets with answers.

All topics

Counting objects, then ones, tens and hundreds, reading big numbers and putting numbers in order. From number bonds and counting on to mental methods and the column method. Equal groups and arrays, every times table, and short and long written methods. Halves and quarters in Year 1 through to adding, multiplying and simplifying fractions in Year 6. Tenths and hundredths, what per cent really means, and how they link to fractions. O'clock and half past, then minutes, Roman numerals and the 24-hour clock. Recognising coins and notes, adding up prices and working out change. Length, mass and capacity, converting units, and perimeter, area and volume. 2D and 3D shapes, angles, symmetry, position, direction and coordinates. Tally charts, pictograms and bar charts, then line graphs, pie charts and the mean. Missing-number problems, sequences, simple formulas, ratio and scaling recipes. The facts to know by heart and the strategies for working the rest out in your head.

Where to start, by age

Every topic runs through primary school, but some matter most at certain ages. If you're not sure where to begin, these are good first stops. Mental maths runs alongside all of them.

AgeGood topics to start with
4 to 6
Reception and Year 1		Counting and place value, addition and subtraction (number bonds to 10, then 20) and shape.
6 to 8
Year 2 and Year 3		Multiplication and division (the 2, 5 and 10 times tables, then 3, 4 and 8), money, telling the time and data and graphs.
8 to 9
Year 4		Multiplication and division (all the tables to 12 × 12, for the Multiplication Tables Check in June), fractions and measurement.
9 to 11
Year 5 and Year 6		Fractions, decimals and percentages, algebra and ratio, and data and graphs, ready for the Key Stage 2 SATs in May of Year 6.
If your child is stuck on a topic, it is often worth going back a step rather than pushing on. A wobble with fractions can come from times tables, and a wobble with column subtraction can come from place value. Each guide's year-by-year table shows what comes before.
