A child may know how to solve a sum but not yet know the word used in the textbook. Start with the idea, then attach the word to it. This glossary explains common primary maths vocabulary; it is not a list that every child must learn by a particular class.

Current NCERT books are called Joyful Mathematics in Classes 1 and 2 and Maths Mela in Classes 3 to 5. Schools using other books may introduce words at different times. Terms such as HCF, LCM and percentages are included for families who encounter them, not as a claim that they belong to every Class 5 course.

Jump to A–C, D–F, G–L, M–P, Q–S or T–Z.

A–C

Abacus
A counting aid with beads arranged by place value. On a place-value abacus, 2 beads in the tens column and 4 in the ones column show 24.
Addition
Putting quantities together, or increasing a quantity. In 8 + 5 = 13, 13 is the sum.
AM and PM
Labels used with a 12-hour clock. 9 AM is in the morning; 9 PM is at night. 12 noon is 12 PM and 12 midnight is 12 AM.
Area
The amount of surface inside a boundary. A rectangle covered by 3 rows of 4 unit squares has an area of 12 square units.
Array
Objects arranged in equal rows and columns. 3 rows of 5 buttons make an array of 15 buttons.
Ascending order
Order from smallest to largest: 7, 12, 19, 25. Descending order goes from largest to smallest.
Bar graph
A display in which bars represent quantities. Read the labels and scale before comparing the bars.
Borrowing
A familiar classroom name for regrouping during subtraction. In 42 − 7, exchange 1 ten for 10 ones: 42 becomes 3 tens and 12 ones, without changing its value.
Bundle
A group treated as one unit. A bundle of 10 sticks represents 1 ten; 10 bundles of ten represent 1 hundred.
Carrying
A familiar classroom name for regrouping during addition. 8 ones + 7 ones = 15 ones, which can be exchanged for 1 ten and 5 ones.
Centimetre
A unit of length, written cm. 100 cm = 1 metre.
Column method
A written calculation with matching place values lined up. Put ones below ones and tens below tens, not simply the ends of the words below one another.
Crore
1,00,00,000: one hundred lakh. Larger Indian number names may be an extension beyond a school's current primary work.

D–F

Data
Information collected for a question, such as the number of children choosing each classroom game.
Decimal
A way of writing numbers using tenths, hundredths and smaller place-value parts. In 2.5, the 5 represents 5 tenths. Follow the school's sequence before introducing decimal calculations.
Denominator
The bottom number in a fraction. In 3/4, the denominator 4 tells us that the whole is divided into 4 equal parts.
Difference
The result of subtraction. The difference between 18 and 11 is 7.
Digit
One of the ten symbols 0–9 used to write numbers. 405 has 3 digits; its middle digit is 0.
Dividend
The quantity being divided. In 24 ÷ 6 = 4, the dividend is 24.
Division
Sharing into equal groups, or finding how many equal-sized groups fit. 24 counters shared among 6 children give 4 each; 24 counters put into groups of 6 make 4 groups.
Divisor
The number we divide by. In 24 ÷ 6 = 4, the divisor is 6.
Equivalent fractions
Fractions with the same value: 1/2 and 2/4 cover the same amount of the same-sized whole.
Estimate
A sensible approximate answer. Before calculating 198 + 203 exactly, use 200 + 200 to estimate 400.
Expanded form
Writing a number as the sum of its place-value parts: 4,205 = 4,000 + 200 + 5.
Face and face value
A face is a surface of a solid shape. Face value is a different expression used in some textbooks for the digit itself: the face value of 7 in 572 is 7, while its place value is 70.
Factor
A whole number that divides another exactly. 3 is a factor of 12 because 12 ÷ 3 = 4 with no remainder.
Fraction
A number that can represent equal parts of a whole or a quantity. 3/4 means three quarters, not 3 unequal pieces out of any 4 pieces.

G–L

Greater than and less than
Comparison words. 9 > 6 reads “9 is greater than 6”; 6 < 9 reads “6 is less than 9”.
Grouping
Making groups of a known size. With 20 counters in groups of 5, there are 4 groups.
HCF
Highest common factor: the greatest factor shared by two numbers. The HCF of 12 and 18 is 6. Treat this as an extension unless the school introduces it.
Kilogram
A unit of mass, written kg. 1 kg = 1,000 grams. Children often hear “weight” in everyday shopping conversations.
Lakh
1,00,000: one hundred thousand. Indian grouping puts the last 3 digits together, then groups digits in pairs: 3,45,672 reads “three lakh forty-five thousand six hundred seventy-two”.
LCM
Lowest common multiple: the smallest positive multiple shared by two numbers. The LCM of 4 and 6 is 12. Its class placement depends on the school's books.
Like fractions
Fractions with the same denominator, such as 2/7 and 5/7. Unlike fractions have different denominators.
Litre
A unit of capacity. 1 litre = 1,000 millilitres.

M–P

Mental maths
Calculating by thinking about number relationships. It need not mean answering instantly or keeping every step unspoken.
Mixed number
A whole number and a fraction written together, such as 1 1/2. Use a drawing before introducing the notation.
Multiple
A number obtained by multiplying a given number by a whole number. 5, 10, 15 and 20 are positive multiples of 5.
Multiplication
Working with equal groups. 4 groups of 6 contain 24 altogether: 4 × 6 = 24.
Number bond
A relationship between a whole and its parts. The bond 7 + 3 = 10 also gives 10 − 7 = 3.
Numerator
The top number in a fraction. In 3/4, the numerator 3 counts the equal parts being considered.
Odd and even
An even whole number can be paired with no item left over. An odd whole number leaves one unpaired item.
Paise
Parts of a rupee: ₹1 = 100 paise. ₹25.50 means 25 rupees and 50 paise.
Percentage
A way of expressing an amount out of 100. 25% means 25 out of 100 and equals 1/4. This may be additional work rather than a current NCERT primary requirement.
Perimeter
The distance around a boundary. A rectangle with sides 6 cm and 4 cm has perimeter 6 + 4 + 6 + 4 = 20 cm.
Pictograph
A display using pictures to represent quantities. If one picture stands for 2 children, 3 pictures represent 6 children.
Place value
The value a digit has because of its position. In 572, 5 means 500, 7 means 70 and 2 means 2.
Predecessor
The whole number immediately before a number. The predecessor of 40 is 39.
Product
The result of multiplication. The product of 6 and 8 is 48.

Q–S

Quotient
The result of division. In 35 ÷ 5 = 7, the quotient is 7.
Reasoning
Explaining why an answer or method makes sense, using words, objects, drawings or a counterexample.
Regrouping
Exchanging between place-value units without changing the quantity: 1 hundred = 10 tens and 1 ten = 10 ones.
Remainder
What is left after making equal whole-number groups. 17 ÷ 5 gives 3 groups with remainder 2, since 3 × 5 + 2 = 17.
Roman numerals
A number-writing system using letters such as I, V and X. Some schools teach it as additional primary work; it is not needed to understand ordinary place value.
Rounding
Replacing a number with a nearby chosen place-value amount. 47 rounded to the nearest ten is 50.
Successor
The whole number immediately after a number. The successor of 99 is 100.
Sum
The result of addition. In 12 + 8 = 20, the sum is 20. In everyday classroom speech, “sums” can also mean calculation exercises generally.
Symmetry
A line of symmetry divides a shape into matching halves that coincide when folded along that line. A rangoli pattern may have symmetry, but not every pattern does.

T–Z

Tables
Multiplication facts, such as the table of 4. Connect recitation to equal groups and arrays.
Tally marks
Marks used to count observations. Each fifth mark crosses the previous four, making groups of 5 easier to read.
Unit
A chosen measure or counting amount. A length answer needs a unit, such as 8 cm; “8” alone does not tell us the length.
Unitary method
Finding the value of one item before finding the value of several. If 3 identical notebooks cost ₹60, one costs ₹20 and 5 cost ₹100. Use only when the same price per item applies.
Zero
A number representing no quantity, and a placeholder in numbers such as 405. 405 has no tens; it is not the same number as 45.
