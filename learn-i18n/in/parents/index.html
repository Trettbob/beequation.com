You do not need to remember every method from your own school days to help your child with maths. You can listen, ask them to show their thinking and give them time to try. A few counters on the table or a question about the clock can be a useful starting point.

These pages are for families of children in Classes 1 to 5, across school boards and teaching mediums. Your child's textbook, maths copy and school diary will tell you what their teacher is working on now. Use that as your starting point: the order of topics and the written methods can differ between schools.

Choose the help you need

Find small opportunities in cooking, shopping, journeys, games and family routines. Make homework and revision manageable, and decide what to do when your child gets stuck. Understand objects, pictures, mental strategies and the written methods in the maths copy. Take pressure out of practice and help your child feel safe enough to attempt a question. Understand classroom checks, report cards and questions to take to a parent–teacher meeting.

Start with what your child understands

Marks tell you something about one piece of work, not everything about a child. Watch how your child counts, chooses an operation or explains an answer. Can they make a number using tens and ones? Can they show why two equal halves make a whole? These conversations help you find a useful next step.

Indie the honeybee likes to show her working with seeds before writing a calculation. Your child can do the same with safe household objects, a drawing or spoken words. They may explain more comfortably in a familiar home language before connecting the idea to the words used at school.

Ask, “Can you show me how you know?” Wait for the explanation before correcting the answer. You may discover that your child understands the idea but has made one counting or writing mistake.
