A child does not have to finish a whole page of sums to have a useful maths session. Counting a handful of beads, explaining one answer or spotting a pattern in a times table can be enough to get started. These free pages help you make those small sessions worthwhile, at home or in the classroom.

Beequation's Learn pages are written for children in Classes 1 to 5 and the adults helping them. There are worked examples, questions to discuss and printable worksheets. Follow the method in your child's notebook where it differs from an example here: schools do not all teach the same topics at the same time.

Indie, our honeybee, suggests five minutes on one idea before moving on. She would rather hear how a child found an answer than see a hurried row of ticks.

Choose the idea you need today

Read numbers and understand what each digit stands for. Combine quantities, find differences and explain carrying over or borrowing. Build equal groups, learn table facts and explore sharing. Work with equal parts of shapes and quantities. Extra help when these ideas appear in the child's school work. Read clocks and connect time with daily routines. Count rupees and paise, read prices and work out change. Explore length, weight, capacity, perimeter and area. Notice sides, corners, faces and symmetry. Sort information, read pictures and compare bars. Start with missing numbers; use later extensions only when needed at school. Choose a strategy rather than guess an answer.

Maths around the house

Ask a child to count out six spoons, compare the lengths of two ribbons or work out how many bananas remain after three are used. When reading a price such as ₹25.50, distinguish rupees from paise. On a journey, notice a bus number or estimate how many steps it takes to reach the gate. The aim is a conversation, not an unexpected test.

Use whichever language helps the child explain the idea. You can connect that explanation to the words used in the school textbook afterwards. Knowing a maths word and understanding the maths are related, but they are not the same task.
