By Class 5, children need to do more than remember a method. They need to decide when it is useful, explain the steps and check whether the result fits the situation. Most are about 10–11. There is still room for counters, folded paper and diagrams alongside written calculations.

Current NCERT Maths Mela develops five-digit numbers, factors, fractions, measurement and information handling. Some schools use books with additional topics or a different order. Treat your child's book and the teacher's instructions as the guide to assessed work.

Read numbers by their places

In 47,306, there are four ten-thousands, seven thousands, three hundreds, no tens and six ones. Write 47,306 = 40,000 + 7,000 + 300 + 6. Compare larger numbers from the highest place downwards rather than looking only at their last digits.

Some schools extend this to six-digit numbers and the Indian number system. If your child's book does, 1,00,000 is one lakh and 1,00,00,000 is one crore. For example, 3,45,672 is three lakh forty-five thousand six hundred seventy-two. These are useful Indian conventions, but lakh and crore work should not be presented as the core five-digit NCERT expectation.

Estimate to check calculations. Four items costing about ₹2,400 each should cost about ₹9,600 altogether. An exact calculation may differ, but it should be in the same sensible range.

Choose and check written methods

Continue addition, subtraction and multiplication with place values aligned. In a word problem, identify the quantity asked for before choosing an operation. A total cost, a difference, a number of groups and an equal share are different questions.

Worked example: 9,876 ÷ 12

Use useful multiples of twelve. Twelve groups of 800 account for 9,600, leaving 276. Twelve groups of 20 account for 240, leaving 36. Twelve groups of 3 account for the remaining 36. Add 800 + 20 + 3 = 823. Therefore 9,876 ÷ 12 = 823.

Check by multiplying: 823 × 12 = 8,230 + 1,646 = 9,876. This partial-quotient approach explains the groups. Your school may record division in a more compact layout; use that layout for schoolwork while retaining the explanation.

When there is a remainder, check that dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder, and that the remainder is smaller than the divisor. Decide what the leftover means in context. Packing children into vehicles may require another vehicle, even when the written division has only a small remainder.

Factors, multiples and prime numbers

A factor divides a number exactly. The factors of twelve are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12. Find them by making rectangles with twelve counters: 1 × 12, 2 × 6 and 3 × 4. Look for factor pairs so none are missed.

The factors of eighteen are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9 and 18. The common factors of twelve and eighteen are 1, 2, 3 and 6. Multiples are different: 12, 24, 36 and 48 are positive multiples of twelve.

A prime number has exactly two positive factors, one and itself. Seven is prime. Eight is composite because it has factors other than one and eight. One is neither prime nor composite. Explore these ideas without automatically adding formal HCF or LCM procedures to the NCERT core.

Ask your child to give an example and explain why it fits. “Seven is prime because…” tells you more than a memorised list of prime numbers.

Use fraction language accurately

In 3/4, the denominator, four, tells us that the whole has four equal parts. The numerator, three, tells us how many of those parts we mean. Keep the same-sized whole when comparing pictures.

Equivalent fractions name the same amount. Multiplying both numerator and denominator by two gives 3/4 = 6/8. To simplify 6/8, divide both by their common factor two to get 3/4. A picture of eighths paired together can show the same change.

Worked example: 1/2 + 1/4

Use pieces of the same size. One half is two quarters, so 1/2 + 1/4 = 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4. Check by shading a half and one more quarter on a single strip. Adding the denominators would change the size of the pieces and would not represent this sum.

For comparison, 2/3 = 8/12 and 3/4 = 9/12, so 3/4 is larger. For a fraction of a quantity, find one part first: three quarters of twenty is three groups of five, or fifteen. Multiplying a fraction by a whole number can also be seen as repeated addition: 3 × 1/4 = 3/4.

Do not assume that every school introduces the same additional fraction procedures in Class 5. If your child's book goes further, ask the teacher which representations and methods they expect.

Area, perimeter and volume

A rectangle 9 m long and 4 m wide has area 9 × 4 = 36 m². Its perimeter is 9 + 4 + 9 + 4 = 26 m. Area measures a surface; perimeter measures a boundary. Write the correct unit so the distinction remains clear.

For a square of side 6 cm, area is 6 × 6 = 36 cm² and perimeter is 4 × 6 = 24 cm. Draw both on a grid and trace the edge before counting the squares inside.

Volume describes space occupied. Build a cuboid with unit cubes: three cubes along, two across and two layers high gives 3 × 2 × 2 = 12 unit cubes. Begin with this model rather than learning a formula without understanding what is counted.

Time, geometry and bar graphs

Read timetables and find durations by counting along a timeline. A journey from 09:45 to 11:20 lasts one hour thirty-five minutes: fifteen minutes to 10:00, one hour to 11:00 and twenty minutes more.

Describe right, acute and obtuse angles, investigate circles and nets, and follow maps with attention to direction and position. Do not turn angle names into a universal requirement for degrees or protractor work unless your child's school introduces it.

On a bar graph, read the title, category labels and numerical scale first. If one interval represents two children, a bar reaching the fourth interval represents eight children, not four. Compare totals and differences, and ask whether the graph contains enough information to answer a question.

Prepare for the next class without rushing

Number patterns, square arrangements, triangular arrangements and magic-square puzzles encourage reasoning. Explain a pattern's rule and check it on more than one step. These habits support the transition to Class 6 better than rushing through every later topic.

Class 5 school assessments are not the same as optional entrance tests or competitions. If your family is considering one, check its separate requirements. For everyday learning, protect time for explanation, reading the question and sensible rough work.
