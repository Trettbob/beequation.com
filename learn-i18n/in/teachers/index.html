A useful maths activity need not require a new set of materials or a separate lesson for every group. Start with a clear mathematical purpose, a familiar example and a question that makes children's thinking visible. These pages offer adaptable ideas for a full class, a small group or a multigrade classroom.

Use the Class labels as starting points, not fixed ability labels. NCERT materials, state-board sequences and school textbooks do not always introduce every topic at the same point. Match an activity to the work children have actually met.

Short prompts with answers and follow-up questions, from counting to fraction reasoning. Low-preparation games using counters, a board, paper and everyday contexts. Adjust support, representation and depth without fixing children into permanent groups. Help children explain, check, compare methods and make sense of word problems.

A simple teaching cycle

  1. Name the goal. “We are learning to add by making a ten” is clearer than “Finish this page”.
  2. Show one example. Use objects, a drawing and an equation where appropriate.
  3. Listen. Ask children what they changed, kept or counted.
  4. Adjust. Offer a bundle of ten sticks, another example or a question that goes deeper.
  5. Choose the next step. Use the explanation, not only the number of finished sums.

For 8 + 5, listen for the child splitting 5 into 2 and 3, making 10 and then 13. A child counting every counter may need a visible group of ten. A child using the bridge confidently can explain why it also helps with 48 + 5.

Keep one question ready that every child can discuss: “How can we check?” It gives a role to a child who finishes early and helps you distinguish a guessed answer from an understood method.

Free resources for a lesson or revision session

The teachers' puzzle pack contains 24 honeycombs: 12 addition puzzles and 12 multiplication puzzles, with an answer key. Use one on the board, let pairs compare equations or keep a few for early finishers. You don't need to time the activity.

The worksheet library offers standard, large-print and easy-read formats, with answers for checking. Choose a small, relevant selection rather than making every child complete all available sheets. Some extension topics are included; their presence does not establish that they belong in every school's primary sequence.

For braille and tactile access, read the braille guidance and check the code, notation and production method with a competent braille reader or special educator before use.

I love hearing two methods for the same puzzle! Take turns: one of you explains, the other checks.

Make home and school explanations work together

At a PTM, show one example from the child's maths copy and explain the mathematical meaning of the school's method. If you use the words carrying or borrowing, connect them to regrouping tens and ones. Parents can then support the same idea without needing to copy a different written procedure from a video.

Suggest brief, familiar practice: a number bond while setting out plates, equal groups of pencils or change from ₹50. Ask parents to tell you what the child explained or found difficult, rather than only how many pages they completed.

Share the maths glossary for plain-language support at home. For information about using Beequation in school, see Beequation for teachers.

Ask, “You have used two of the five counters to reach ten. How many are left?” Three are left because 5 − 2 = 3. Then 8 + 2 + 3 = 13. Let the child check the split with five objects.
