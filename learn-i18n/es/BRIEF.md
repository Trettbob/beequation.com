## 1. The school system

| Stage | Years (local names) | Ages | Compulsory? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Educación Infantil, primer ciclo | "0-3" (escuela infantil / guardería) | 0 to 3 | No |
| Educación Infantil, segundo ciclo | "Infantil 3 años", "Infantil 4 años", "Infantil 5 años" (also "1.º, 2.º y 3.º de Infantil"; in Catalonia "P3, P4, P5"; other regions' local names UNCERTAIN) | 3 to 6 | No ("carácter voluntario", RD 95/2022 art. 5), but free (gratuito, LOE art. 15.2) and almost every child attends |
| Educación Primaria | 1.º, 2.º, 3.º, 4.º, 5.º, 6.º de Primaria, in three "ciclos" of two years: primer ciclo (1.º–2.º), segundo ciclo (3.º–4.º), tercer ciclo (5.º–6.º) | 6 to 12 | Yes |
| Educación Secundaria Obligatoria (ESO) | 1.º to 4.º de la ESO | 12 to 16 | Yes |

- School becomes compulsory with **1.º de Primaria**: "la enseñanza básica… se desarrolla… entre los seis y los dieciséis
  años" (LOE art. 4.2). Children start 1.º "en el año natural en el que cumplan seis años" (RD 157/2022 art. 3).
- **The cut-off is 31 December** (calendar year of birth), not 1 September as in England. A class is all the children
  born in one calendar year. So in 1.º de Primaria children are 5 years 8 months to 6 years 8 months in September,
  and the year is described as "de 6 a 7 años". Spanish children are on average about 8 months older than English
  children in the same-numbered Year, and one year group "behind" by number: 1.º de Primaria ≈ English Year 2 by age.
- Writing years: "1.º de Primaria" (with the ordinal indicator "º" after a full stop, as the RAE recommends), short
  "1.º", "2.º"…; "primero de Primaria" in speech. Infantil years are named by age: "Infantil 5 años" (also "el último
  curso de Infantil", "3.º de Infantil", "P5" in Catalonia). "Primaria" and "Infantil" are capitalised as stage names.
- Teachers: "maestro / maestra" (Primaria and Infantil teachers are "maestros"; "profesor" is also used),
  "el tutor / la tutora" (class teacher), "el cole" / "el colegio" (school), "CEIP" (public infant+primary school),
  "colegio concertado" (state-funded private, often religious), "colegio privado".
- The ministry sets the "enseñanzas mínimas" (minimum content). **Each comunidad autónoma writes its own decree**
  with the full curriculum (e.g. Madrid Decreto 61/2022; Andalucía Orden de 30 de mayo de 2023; Catalonia Decret
  175/2022), and each school and textbook turns that into a course-by-course programme ("programación"). In
  Catalonia, the Basque Country, Galicia, Valencia, the Balearics and Navarre maths may be taught in the co-official
  language. Our pages are in Castilian Spanish and should say "depende de tu comunidad autónoma y del colegio" where
  practice varies.

### Years our Learn section covers

The English section covers ages 4/5 to 11 (end of pre-school to end of primary). The Spanish one covers
**Infantil 5 años and 1.º to 6.º de Primaria** (ages 5 to 12): the last pre-school year and the whole of Primaria.

### School calendar and reports

- The regional "calendario escolar" sets the dates; at least 175 school days (RD 157/2022, disposición adicional tercera).
  Primaria usually starts in the **first or second week of September** and ends **around 19 to 23 June**.
  Holidays: Navidad (about 22 December to 7 January, with Reyes on 6 January), Semana Santa (March/April), summer
  (late June to early September), plus regional and local holidays. Many regions have "jornada intensiva"
  (mornings only) in September and June. (Exact dates vary by region: say "septiembre" / "finales de junio".)
- The year is split into three "trimestres" or "evaluaciones"; families get a report ("boletín de notas", "informe")
  each term. Grades in Primaria are words: **Insuficiente (IN), Suficiente (SU), Bien (BI), Notable (NT),
  Sobresaliente (SB)** (RD 157/2022 art. 26). Infantil reports are descriptive, with no grades.
- Promotion is decided at the end of each ciclo (2.º, 4.º, 6.º), and repeating a year ("repetir curso") is exceptional
  (art. 15). At the end of each ciclo the tutor writes a report on the key competences ("competencias clave").

## 2. What the curriculum expects, year by year

**How the official curriculum is built.** The national text (RD 157/2022) gives maths content ("saberes básicos")
**per ciclo, not per year**, organised in six "sentidos": **numérico, de la medida, espacial, algebraico,
estocástico** (data and probability) and **socioafectivo** (attitudes, emotions, teamwork). Problem solving
("resolución de problemas") is the first of five axes. Infantil has no maths subject: maths sits inside the area
"Descubrimiento y Exploración del Entorno" (RD 95/2022).

Per-year detail below combines: (a) the national ciclo content (quoted), (b) the Comunidad de Madrid per-course
guide (Plan de Rescate, 2025), the only official per-course list found, and (c) usual textbook practice, marked
UNCERTAIN. When writing a year page, present (a) as "lo que marca el currículo para el ciclo" and (b)/(c) as "lo
que se suele trabajar en este curso". Don't claim one region's split is national.

### Infantil 5 años (5 a 6 años)

- **Official (RD 95/2022, segundo ciclo, whole cycle 3 to 6):** "Cualidades o atributos de objetos y materiales.
  Relaciones de orden, correspondencia, clasificación y comparación"; "Cuantificadores básicos contextualizados";
  "Funcionalidad de los números en la vida cotidiana"; "Situaciones en las que se hace necesario medir"; "Nociones
  espaciales básicas"; "El tiempo y su organización: día-noche, estaciones, ciclos, calendario…"; programming
  sequences of instructions (pensamiento computacional).
- **Madrid guide (2.º ciclo de Infantil):** cardinal and ordinal numbers and "las operaciones" (drawn and spoken);
  "aproximación a la serie numérica"; quantifiers ("igual que, más que, menos que, tantos como, muchos, pocos,
  alguno, ninguno"); spatial words ("dentro-fuera, encima-debajo, cerca-lejos… izquierda-derecha"); size words
  ("grande-mediano-pequeño, largo-corto, alto-bajo, pesado-ligero"); measuring with hands, feet and steps; series
  and patterns ("seriación"); "añadir, quitar, repartir".
- **Usual practice (UNCERTAIN, varies by school and textbook):** counting objects to 10 and reciting further (often
  to 20 or beyond); recognising and writing the digits 0 to 9 ("la grafía de los números"); adding and taking away
  with objects to about 10; circle, square, triangle, rectangle; sorting; simple patterns; days of the week.
- No numbers are set for "number bonds" or written sums; there is no test.

### Primer ciclo: 1.º and 2.º de Primaria

**National (RD 157/2022, primer ciclo):**
- Numbers: counting and reading/writing natural numbers **up to 999**, "incluida la recta numérica"; composing
  and decomposing; "Sistema de numeración de base diez (hasta el 999)"; comparing and ordering.
- Operations: "Estrategias de cálculo mental con números naturales hasta 999"; "Suma y resta… resueltas con
  flexibilidad y sentido"; the relation between addition and subtraction. **Multiplication is not in the national
  primer ciclo minimum** (it starts in segundo ciclo, see below).
- Money: "monedas (1, 2 euros) y billetes de euro (5, 10, 20, 50 y 100), valor y equivalencia".
- Measurement: length, mass, capacity; "metro, kilo y litro" and non-standard units; time "año, mes, semana, día y
  hora"; measuring with rulers, tapes, scales, calendars.
- Shape: simple 2D shapes in everyday objects, building 1D/2D/3D shapes, basic vocabulary, position words
  ("arriba, abajo, delante, detrás, entre, más cerca que…").
- Algebra: patterns and missing elements in sequences; "=" and "≠"; a missing number shown by a symbol
  (4 + ■ = 10); following simple algorithms (pensamiento computacional).
- Data: reading pictograms and bar charts ("pictogramas, gráficas de barras"), collecting and counting small data
  sets, drawing simple charts.

**1.º de Primaria (6 a 7 años).** Madrid guide: count, read and write **to 99**; tens and ones ("equivalencias entre
decenas y unidades"); mental addition and subtraction to 99; odd and even; "anterior, intermedio y posterior";
addition and subtraction and their terms; metre and kilo plus "dedos, palmos, pies, pasos"; year, month, week, day,
hour; simple 2D shapes; position words; simple charts.
Usual practice (UNCERTAIN): sums to 10 and then to 20 ("amigos del 10", doubles); column addition without
carrying, then with carrying late in the year; subtraction without carrying; o'clock and half past ("en punto",
"y media"); coins and notes; doubles and halves.

**2.º de Primaria (7 a 8 años).** Madrid guide: count, read and write **to 999**; hundreds, tens and ones; mental
calculation to 999; addition and subtraction to 999; ordinals to "décimo"; "=", "≠", ">" and "<";
"Sistema monetario europeo"; metre, kilo and litre; "año, mes, semana, día, hora, minuto, segundo"; simple 2D and
3D shapes.
Usual practice (UNCERTAIN, but very common in Spanish textbooks): column addition and subtraction **"llevando"**
(with carrying); **multiplication introduced as repeated addition and the first "tablas de multiplicar"**, often
all of them from 1 to 10 by the end of 2.º or during 3.º; "doble, triple, mitad"; sharing ("repartir") as the first
idea of division; quarter past/to ("y cuarto", "menos cuarto"); euros and cents; "medio" and "cuarto" in everyday
contexts.
Note: the Madrid guide lists only addition and subtraction for 1.º and 2.º; multiplication and division first appear
in its 3.º list, and "Conoce y construye las tablas de multiplicar" is a segundo ciclo indicator. So tables in 2.º
are common textbook practice, not an official requirement: write "en muchos colegios se empiezan en 2.º".

### Segundo ciclo: 3.º and 4.º de Primaria

**National (RD 157/2022, segundo ciclo):**
- Numbers **up to 9999**; "decenas, centenas y millares"; estimating and rounding ("aproximaciones").
- "**Fracciones propias con denominador hasta 12** en contextos de la vida cotidiana"; comparing and ordering
  natural numbers and fractions.
- Operations: mental calculation with natural numbers and fractions; "suma, resta, multiplicación, división como
  reparto y partición"; "**Construcción de las tablas de multiplicar** apoyándose en número de veces, suma repetida o
  disposición en cuadrículas"; all four operations "con flexibilidad y sentido"; inverse relations.
- Money: "euros y céntimos de euro… ingresos, gastos y ahorro. Decisiones de compra responsable".
- Measurement: length, mass, capacity, area, volume and angle as attributes; "km, m, cm, mm; kg, g; l y ml";
  converting to smaller units; time "año, mes, semana, día, hora y minutos" and durations; "reloj analógico y digital".
- Shape: 2D and 3D shapes and their elements; ruler and set square ("regla y escuadra"); "paralelo, perpendicular,
  oblicuo"; routes on plans; translations and symmetry ("traslaciones y simetrías"); **perimeter**.
- Algebra: patterns and predicting terms; "=", "≠", "<", ">"; missing numbers shown by a symbol; modifying simple
  algorithms (block programming, robotics).
- Data: reading pictograms, bar charts and histograms; absolute frequency; **the mode** ("la moda"); probability as
  "suceso seguro, posible e imposible".
- **Decimals are not in the national segundo ciclo minimum** (they appear in tercer ciclo, "hasta las milésimas");
  money in "euros y céntimos" is the only decimal-like content. The Madrid guide adds "Los números decimales" in 4.º,
  and its segundo ciclo indicators include "Realiza sumas y restas sencillas con números decimales" and reading
  proper "e impropias" fractions. Present decimals in 4.º as "en muchas comunidades, como Madrid", not as national.

**3.º de Primaria (8 a 9 años).** Madrid guide: count, read and write **to 9.999**; multiples of 5, 25 and 50;
**Roman numerals** ("numeración romana"); additive and multiplicative decomposition; place value names up to
"unidades de millar" (and "décimas, centésimas y milésimas" listed as equivalences); "=", "≠", "<", ">";
**addition, subtraction, multiplication and division** and their terms; euro equivalences; the full metric ladder
"km, hm, dam, m, dm, cm, mm; t, kg, hg, dag, g, dg, cg, mg; kl, hl, dal, l, dl, cl y ml" with abbreviations; time and
durations; classifying 2D and 3D shapes; turning and direction words; reading everyday charts.
Usual practice (UNCERTAIN): all tables to 10 secure; multiplying by one and then two digits; division by a one-digit
divisor in the "casita" layout; first fractions (half, third, quarter… numerator and denominator); perimeter;
angles (recto, agudo, obtuso); reading the clock to the minute and the digital clock.

**4.º de Primaria (9 a 10 años).** Madrid guide: count, read and write **to 99.999**; Roman numerals; ordinals to
"trigésimo"; place value to "decena de millar"; **decimal numbers** ("los números decimales"); **proper fractions with
denominator up to 12**, comparing, ordering and equivalences; all four operations; "equivalencias entre euros y
céntimos"; the metric ladder; time to seconds and durations; 2D/3D shapes; movements, turns and symmetry.
Madrid's segundo ciclo indicators also list simple addition and subtraction of decimals.
Usual practice (UNCERTAIN): division by two-digit divisors; tenths and hundredths ("décimas y centésimas") linked to
cents; fractions of an amount (¾ de 20); area by counting squares; the
**evaluación de diagnóstico** this year (see section 3).

### Tercer ciclo: 5.º and 6.º de Primaria

**National (RD 157/2022, tercer ciclo):**
- Natural numbers and **decimals to thousandths** ("hasta las milésimas"); fractions and decimals to express
  amounts; mental, written and calculator methods with naturals, decimals and fractions.
- "**Potencia** como producto de factores iguales. **Cuadrados y cubos**"; "**múltiplos y divisores**";
  "Relación entre fracciones sencillas, decimales y **porcentajes**".
- "Razonamiento proporcional": proportional and non-proportional situations; "problemas de proporcionalidad,
  porcentajes y escalas… mediante la igualdad entre razones, la reducción a la unidad o el uso de coeficientes de
  proporcionalidad".
- Money: "consumo responsable (valor/precio, calidad/precio y mejor precio)… precios, intereses y rebajas".
- Measurement: the "Sistema Métrico Decimal" for length, mass, capacity, volume and area; time; **degrees** for
  angles; the link between the metric system and the decimal number system.
- Shape: classifying shapes; compass and protractor ("compás y transportador de ángulos"); maps, plans, scales and
  "puntos cardinales"; "primer cuadrante del sistema de coordenadas cartesiano"; rotations, translations,
  reflections; similar shapes ("semejanza"); **areas and perimeters** of plane shapes.
- Algebra: creating and predicting patterns; unknowns "representados por medio de una letra o un símbolo" with
  "=", "≠", "<", ">"; creating algorithms with loops and conditionals.
- Data: simple statistical studies; tables of absolute and relative frequency; bar charts, **pie charts**
  ("diagrama de sectores") and histograms; **mean and mode** ("media y moda"); **range** ("rango"); spreadsheets;
  probability with **Laplace's rule** ("regla de Laplace").

**5.º de Primaria (10 a 11 años).** Madrid guide: natural numbers, integers ("enteros") and decimals to thousandths;
operations with naturals, fractions and decimals; **prime and composite numbers**; powers of 10, squares and cubes;
multiples and divisors with **divisibility rules for 2, 5 and 10**; fractions: equivalence, simplifying,
"amplificación", ordering with the same denominator; consumer problems (prices, interest, discounts); the metric
system for length, capacity, mass, volume and area; degrees; plans and maps; areas and perimeters; bar charts,
pie charts and histograms.
Usual practice (UNCERTAIN): numbers to millions; adding and subtracting fractions; multiplying and dividing
decimals; percentages introduced (e.g. 25 % de 80 = 20); angles measured with a protractor; area of triangles and
parallelograms.

**6.º de Primaria (11 a 12 años).** Madrid guide: integers; **π**; all operations with naturals, fractions and
decimals; powers; **divisibility rules for 2, 3, 5 and 10; "mínimo común múltiplo" (m.c.m.) and "máximo común
divisor" (m.c.d.)**; reducing fractions to a common denominator; fractions, decimals and percentages; **proportionality
problems ("regla de tres", "reducción a la unidad"), percentages and scales**; consumer problems; metric units,
time and degrees; areas, perimeters **and volumes**; finding unknowns shown by a letter or symbol.
Usual practice (UNCERTAIN): negative numbers on a number line and in temperatures; square roots; circle and
circumference (radius, diameter, length of circumference); mean, mode, range; simple probability.

- **There is no national times-tables test** (nothing like a timed multiplication check), no national test in 2.º,
  and no entrance test for secondary: children move to ESO at their local "instituto".
- Some regions have used other tests in the past (e.g. LOMCE's 3.º and 6.º "evaluaciones individualizadas", now
  gone). Don't describe old LOMCE tests as current.
- The national framework talks about "situaciones de aprendizaje" (learning situations) and "competencias
  específicas" with "criterios de evaluación"; parents won't know these words, teachers will.

## 4. Written methods ("algoritmos") and how they are laid out

Spanish children call written calculations "**cuentas**" or "**operaciones**"; teachers say "**algoritmo**" and
"colocar bien la cuenta" (line the digits up). Two families of methods exist side by side:

1. **"Algoritmos tradicionales" (traditional written algorithms, CBC: "cerrados, basados en cifras")**, still the most
   common. Vertical layout, units under units ("unidades debajo de unidades"), worked right to left.
2. **Método ABN** ("Algoritmos Abiertos Basados en Números"), created by Jaime Martínez Montero and spread from
   schools in Cádiz from about 2008 (dates UNCERTAIN); used in many schools, especially in Andalucía, and in some
   classes elsewhere. Works with whole numbers rather than digits, flexibly, often in a grid ("rejilla" / "tabla").
   It is a school choice, not part of the official curriculum: the curriculum only asks for operations "con
   flexibilidad y sentido".
   Pages should explain both and tell parents to ask which one the school uses, and not to teach "the other way" at
   home without checking.

Also used in some schools: **Método Singapur** (bar models), **Entusiasmat**, **regletas Cuisenaire**, ábaco,
bloques multibase. Prevalence UNCERTAIN; mention as examples only.

### Addition: "suma llevando" ("con llevadas")

```
   1 1          ← "las llevadas", written small above
   3 4 7
 + 2 8 5
 -------
   6 3 2
```
Said aloud: "7 más 5, 12; pongo el 2 y **me llevo una**. 4 más 8, 12, y una que me llevaba, 13; pongo el 3 y me llevo
una. 3 más 2, 5, y una, 6." Result 632. Terms: "sumandos", "suma" or "total".

### Subtraction: "resta llevando" (traditional = equal additions / compensation)

The traditional Spanish method is **not** "borrowing" from the top digit. When the top digit is too small, 10 is
added to it, and **1 is added to the next digit of the bottom number** ("me llevo una"); children usually count up
from the bottom digit ("de 8 a 12 van 4"):
```
   5 3 2
 − 1 7 8      (small 1s are added next to the 7 and the 1 of the bottom number)
 -------
   3 5 4
```
"De 8 a 12 van 4 y me llevo una. 7 y una, 8; de 8 a 13 van 5 y me llevo una. 1 y una, 2; de 2 a 5 van 3." Result 354
(check: 354 + 178 = 532, "la prueba de la resta"). Terms: "minuendo", "sustraendo", "diferencia" (or "resta").
Many current textbooks instead teach **"desagrupar" / "pedir prestado"** (exchange a ten from the top number,
crossing it out), which is what English schools do. Which one a school uses: UNCERTAIN, varies; pages should show
both and say the answer is the same.

### Multiplication

```
     2 3 4
   ×   2 3
   -------
     7 0 2      ← 234 × 3
   4 6 8        ← 234 × 2 (tens), moved one place left (gap or 0 at the end)
   -------
   5 3 8 2
```
Terms: "factores" (also "multiplicando" and "multiplicador"), "producto". "Llevadas" also happen here. Times tables are
said "tres por cuatro, doce" and written 3 × 4 = 12.

### Division: "la división en casita" (also "la caja")

Dividend on the left, divisor on the right inside a corner ⌊‾, quotient written **under the divisor**, remainders
under the dividend. From 3.º or 4.º, children usually **do the subtraction mentally and write only the remainder**:
```
 8 5 7 |_4____
 0 5     2 1 4
   1 7
     1
```
"8 entre 4, a 2; 2 por 4, 8; de 8 a 8, 0. Bajo el 5: 5 entre 4, a 1; 1 por 4, 4; de 4 a 5, 1. Bajo el 7: 17 entre 4, a
4; 4 por 4, 16; de 16 a 17, 1." Quotient 214, remainder 1. Check ("la prueba de la división"): **dividendo = divisor ×
cociente + resto**: 4 × 214 + 1 = 857. Terms: "dividendo", "divisor", "cociente", "resto". A division is "exacta"
(resto 0) or "entera" (resto ≠ 0). At first, children write the subtractions out in full ("con restas").
England's "bus stop" layout, grid method and chunking are not used in Spain; don't show them.

### ABN in one example

47 + 38 in a grid: take 3 from 38 and give them to 47, making 50 and 35, then 50 + 35 = 85. Subtraction often by
"escalera" (counting up) or by taking away the same amount from both; division by building up the quotient with
easy multiples ("por tablas"). Keep ABN examples simple and correct.

## 5. Notation

- **Decimal comma**: 2,5 (read "dos coma cinco"; in school "2 unidades y 5 décimas"). Never a point for decimals.
- **Thousands**: textbooks and the Madrid guide write a **point**: 1.000, 9.999, 25.000 (RAE prefers a thin space and
  no separator in 4-digit numbers, but parents see points in school books). Use a point for 5+ digit numbers and in
  4-digit numbers in number-range statements like "hasta el 9.999"; never in years (2026), page numbers or times.
  Suggested rule for all pages: point from 1.000 upwards, except years.
- **Multiplication**: "×" in Primaria (the "aspa", read "por"); "·" appears in ESO. Don't use "*".
- **Division**: in a line, **":"** (12 : 3 = 4, read "doce entre tres") is the usual school sign; "÷" is understood
  (calculators) and the Madrid guide itself heads its word bank "DIVISIÓN ÷", so both appear in school material
  (which is more common in textbooks: UNCERTAIN). Written division uses the "casita".
  Recommended for our pages: ":" in line, and say once per page where it matters that it is also written "÷"
  (e.g. "12 : 3 = 4, que también verás escrito 12 ÷ 3").
- **Minus**: "−", read "menos". **Equals**: "=", read "igual a" or "son" (3 + 4 son 7).
- **Comparison**: "<" "menor que", ">" "mayor que", "≠" "distinto de / no es igual a" (all in the official curriculum).
- **Fractions**: stacked ½, ¾ (fraction characters are fine); read "un medio, dos tercios, tres cuartos, un quinto,
  un sexto, un séptimo, un octavo, un noveno, un décimo", then "-avos": "onceavos, doceavos". Mixed numbers: "2 y ½"
  or "2 enteros y un medio" ("números mixtos"). "Fracción propia / impropia".
- **Percent**: "25 %" with a (non-breaking) space, read "veinticinco por ciento".
- **Ordinals**: 1.º, 2.º, 1.ª (with the full stop).
- **Units**: metric only. Symbols without full stops: m, cm, mm, km, kg, g, l (also L), ml, t; "h" and "min" for
  hours and minutes. Measurement ladder: km, hm, dam, m, dm, cm, mm (taught as "la escalera").
- **Money**: "3,45 €" (symbol after the amount, with a space); read "tres euros con cuarenta y cinco (céntimos)" or
  informally "tres cuarenta y cinco". Cents: "45 céntimos" or "0,45 €"; "cent." is used on price labels.
- **Times**: digital and 24-hour times written **15:30** (also "15.30 h"; "15:30 h" common on timetables).
- **Large numbers**: "un millón" = 1.000.000; "un billón" in Spanish = a million million (10¹²), not the English
  billion. "Mil millones" = 10⁹. Important on any page that mentions large numbers.

## 6. Money

- Euro coins: **1, 2, 5, 10, 20 and 50 céntimos; 1 € and 2 €**. Notes: **5, 10, 20, 50, 100 and 200 €** (500 € notes
  are no longer issued but remain legal tender: leave them out). 100 céntimos = 1 euro.
- Spanish 1, 2 and 5 cent coins are copper-coloured; Spain's coins show the Cathedral of Santiago, Cervantes and the
  King (detail not needed on pages).
- Everyday contexts: "la paga" (pocket money), "la hucha" (money box), buying "chuches" (sweets) at "el quiosco",
  bread at "la panadería" (una barra de pan), fruit at "la frutería" or "el mercado", stationery at "la papelería",
  "el súper". Use plausible prices and don't name real chains.

## 7. Time

- Analogue: 3:00 "**las tres en punto**", 3:15 "**las tres y cuarto**", 3:30 "**las tres y media**",
  3:45 "**las cuatro menos cuarto**", 3:10 "las tres y diez", 3:35 "las cuatro menos veinticinco", 3:50 "las cuatro
  menos diez". "De la mañana / de la tarde / de la noche / de la madrugada"; "mediodía" (12:00), "medianoche" (00:00).
  Note: "la una" (1:00), "es la una y media" but "son las dos".
- **24-hour time is everyday Spanish** (TV, trains, school timetables: "a las 17:00", "las diecisiete horas"). Schools
  teach the digital clock and the link 15:00 = las tres de la tarde in **primer or segundo ciclo** (the national
  curriculum names "reloj analógico y digital" in segundo ciclo; usual practice is 2.º–3.º, UNCERTAIN).
- Spanish daily rhythm, useful for examples: school often 9:00 to 14:00 or 9:00 to 16:00/17:00 with "comedor";
  "el recreo" (break, about 11:00–11:30), "la merienda" (afternoon snack, about 17:30), late dinner (about 21:00).
- Dates are written day/month/year: 10/10/2026, "10 de octubre de 2026". The week starts on **Monday** (lunes).

## 9. Form of address and tone

- **"Tú"**, as in our apps (`apps/kids/src/i18n/es.ts`), store listings and `i18n/es.json`: "tu hijo o hija",
  "juega gratis en tu navegador", "pruébalo". Never "usted".
- **No "vosotros"** forms: our store copy avoids them on purpose (shared with Latin American readers). Instead of
  "podéis jugar juntos", write "puedes jugar con tu hijo", "jugad" → "jugar en familia".
- Parents: "**las familias**" is the usual school word ("Para familias"); "tu hijo o hija" once per section, then
  "tu hijo", "el niño", "los niños" as generic. Avoid heavy doubling ("los/las"). Teachers: "tu clase", "tus
  alumnos", "el alumnado"; address teachers as "tú" too ("Para docentes").
- Warm, direct, plain: a Spanish "maestra" talking to a parent at the school gate. No "simplemente", "fácil",
  "sencillísimo". No emoji. Use the words children hear in class, then explain them ("lo que en clase llaman…").
- Spanish punctuation: opening ¿ and ¡; quotes «…» or "…" (pick one per page; «» is more Spanish-typographic,
  `i18n/es.json` uses "…" in places — UNCERTAIN which the site prefers; be consistent).
