## 1. Which programme applies now (2026-2027)

All the primary years now follow **new programmes**, written year by year (no longer only per cycle):

| Classes | Text | In force since |
|---|---|---|
| Maternelle (PS, MS, GS) | BO n° 41, 31 Oct 2024 | rentrée 2025 |
| CP, CE1, CE2 | BO n° 41, 31 Oct 2024 | rentrée 2025 |
| CM1 (and 6e) | BO n° 16, 17 Apr 2025 | rentrée 2025 |
| CM2 | BO n° 16, 17 Apr 2025 | **rentrée 2026** (this school year is its first) |

So every page describes the 2024/2025 programmes. Don't describe the 2015/2018/2020 programmes (no "attendus de
fin de cycle" as the main reference, no "repères de progressivité" tables).

Big features of the new programmes worth knowing (they shape every page):

- Cycle 2: **at least two thirds of maths time** goes to « Nombres, calcul et résolution de problèmes ».
- **Daily** mental maths sessions (« des séances quotidiennes de calcul mental ») through cycle 2, with **timed
  fluency tests** (« fluence en calcul ») and target numbers per year (table in section 4).
- **No calculator** in cycle 2 (except for pupils with special needs). In CM1/CM2 pupils have no personal
  calculator; the teacher may hand some out for particular activities.
- Explicit teaching of problem solving with a **four-phase model**: « Comprendre », « Modéliser », « Calculer »,
  « Répondre » (plus a « Régulation » step: is the answer plausible?). Named problem types: « problèmes additifs »
  of the « parties-tout » and « comparaison » kinds, multiplicative « parties-tout », « comparaison
  multiplicative », two- and three-step problems, « partage équitable » and « groupement ».
- **Bar models are official**: « schéma en barre » (and « schéma avec deux barres », « déplacement sur un axe »)
  from CE1 onwards as a modelling tool. This is the Singapore-style bar model.
- The concrete → pictorial → abstract idea is written in: manipulate (« manipulation »), represent
  (« représentation schématisée »), then the mathematical language, with verbalisation all the way.
- CM1 and CM2: **at least 10 problems a week**, some of them short oral ones answered on the slate (« ardoise »).
- New in cycle 3: an « Algèbre » strand (« pensée algébrique »: missing numbers, symbols or letters for
  unknowns, « programme de calcul », number and pattern sequences), **probability** (« probabilités ») in CM1 and
  CM2, and an « Initiation à la pensée informatique » strand.
- Each year's text says *when* things start, by school period (« période 1 » to « période 5 », the five blocks
  between holidays): e.g. « la centaine sera abordée dès la première période du CE1 ».

Weekly time (arrêté of 9 Nov 2015): 24 hours of school a week; **maths 5 hours a week** in cycle 2 and cycle 3
(180 hours a year).

## 2. The school system

| Stage | Class (name used by everyone) | Full name | Age during the year | Cycle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| École maternelle | PS | petite section | 3 to 4 | cycle 1 |
| École maternelle | MS | moyenne section | 4 to 5 | cycle 1 |
| École maternelle | **GS** | grande section | **5 to 6** | cycle 1 |
| École élémentaire | **CP** | cours préparatoire | **6 to 7** | cycle 2 |
| École élémentaire | **CE1** | cours élémentaire 1re année | **7 to 8** | cycle 2 |
| École élémentaire | **CE2** | cours élémentaire 2e année | **8 to 9** | cycle 2 |
| École élémentaire | **CM1** | cours moyen 1re année | **9 to 10** | cycle 3 |
| École élémentaire | **CM2** | cours moyen 2e année | **10 to 11** | cycle 3 |
| Collège | 6e | sixième | 11 to 12 | cycle 3 |
| Collège | 5e, 4e, 3e | | 12 to 15 | cycle 4 |

- École maternelle + école élémentaire = « l'école primaire » (teachers also say « le premier degré »). Some
  schools are combined (« groupe scolaire », « école primaire »), others separate.
- **School is compulsory from age 3** (« instruction obligatoire dès l'âge de trois ans », Code de l'éducation,
  article L131-1, since the rentrée 2019) to 16.
- Children go into a class by **calendar year of birth** (1 January to 31 December), not by an autumn cut-off. A
  child born in 2020 starts CP in September 2026, so ages run from just under 6 to 7 in CP.
- Cycles: cycle 1 = maternelle; cycle 2 = « cycle des apprentissages fondamentaux » (CP–CE2); cycle 3 = « cycle de
  consolidation » (CM1, CM2 and 6e at collège).
- Teacher: « le professeur des écoles », called « la maîtresse / le maître » by children and many parents
  (« l'enseignant(e) » in neutral writing). Head: « le directeur / la directrice d'école ».
- Classes are often mixed-age (« classe à double niveau », e.g. CE2-CM1), especially in rural schools.

**Our Learn section covers GS to CM2** (ages 5 to 11): the last year of maternelle and the five years of
élémentaire. That is six year pages: `grande-section`, `cp`, `ce1`, `ce2`, `cm1`, `cm2`.

How they line up with the English pages: by age, GS = Year 1, CP = Year 2 … CM2 = Year 6; but by content, French
classes sit about a year "behind" the English age (CP works on numbers to 100 like Year 1; CE1 reaches 1,000 and
all the times tables). Writers should adapt by content, never compare with England on the page.

## 3. What each year learns (official programme)

Number ranges at a glance: GS counts to 30, numbers to 10 « voire au-delà »; CP to **100**; CE1 to **1 000**;
CE2 to **10 000**; CM1 to **999 999**; CM2 to **999 999 999**.

### Grande section (GS, age 5 to 6), cycle 1

The cycle 1 text is organised by age (« avant 4 ans », « à partir de 4 ans », « à partir de 5 ans ou dès que les
apprentissages précédents ont pu être observés »). The "from 5" items, i.e. GS:

- **Numbers and counting** (« Découvrir les nombres »): understand that each number is the previous one plus one;
  recognise and make collections of the same « cardinal » up to ten « voire au-delà », whatever the size, colour or
  layout of the objects; count (« dénombrer ») by the « principe de cardinalité »; compare quantities (« plus que »,
  « moins que », « autant que »), by one-to-one matching and by counting; read and **write numerals 1 to 10**;
  **recite the counting rhyme (« comptine numérique ») to thirty**, from any starting number, backwards from 10 to 1,
  and in twos to 20; ordinal position (« premier », « dernier », « deuxième », « avant-dernier »), rank in a row of up
  to ten; build and fill in a **« bande numérique » to ten**.
- **Number bonds and early calculation**: compose and decompose numbers to ten (with the fingers of both hands
  too), and say them: « quatre et quatre font huit », doubles (« cinq et cinq font dix », « deux fois cinq font
  dix »); count on from the larger number to add (« surcompter »); zero is not introduced early (it may come up in
  a take-away problem).
- **Problems**: part-whole (« parties-tout »), adding or removing, moves on a track (« jeu de l'oie », number
  track), comparing, grouping, **fair sharing** (« partage équitable », possibly with a remainder), using a drawing.
- **Shape**: describe cube, pavé (cuboid), boule, pyramids, cylindre, cône; name carré, rectangle, triangle,
  disque in any orientation; sort and classify shapes; reproduce assemblies, puzzles, tilings; use a ruler to draw.
- **Measures** (« Explorer des grandeurs »): compare lengths indirectly with a strip (« bande témoin »), order up to
  five lengths; compare and order three masses, « balance de type Roberval », « plus lourd que », « plus léger
  que », « de même masse que ». (No time, money, or units in the cycle 1 maths text.)
- **Patterns** (« motifs organisés »): repeating and growing patterns (e.g. ABAABBAAABBB), describe, transcribe,
  create and continue them. This is the GS seed of early algebra.
- Assessment: none national (see section 4).

### CP (age 6 to 7), cycle 2

- **Numbers**: whole numbers **to 100** (to 59 by period 2 at the latest, to 100 by period 3); tens and ones from
  period 1, with « cubes emboîtables », « bûchettes », « matériel multibase », play money (1 € coins, 10 € notes);
  read and write numerals, **number words written in letters to fifty**; « unités de numération » (« trois dizaines
  et cinq unités »), 30 + 5; compare with **=, < and >**; order five numbers; place numbers on a **« demi-droite
  graduée »** (and a « bande numérique »); ordinals to « vingtième ».
- **Addition and subtraction**: meaning of + − =; subtraction as the inverse of addition (32 + 15 = 47 so
  47 − 32 = 15); « = » only between equal things (« 47 + 3 = 50 + 5 = 55 » is wrong). **Written column addition
  (« addition posée en colonnes ») only from period 4 or 5**, two or three numbers of one or two digits (45 + 37,
  28 + 8 + 56). Subtraction by manipulating and breaking a ten (« cassage de dizaines ») in problems from period 3.
  No written subtraction method yet.
- **Multiplication**: meaning only, through repeated addition and the word « fois » (« trois fois vingt » written
  20 + 20 + 20). No times tables yet, no × sign in the CP text.
- **Division**: only as fair-sharing and grouping problems.
- **Mental maths**: the addition facts both ways (« tables d'addition », A + B with A and B from 0 to 10, missing
  numbers « 4 + … = 12 »: target **8 a minute** by the end of CP); doubles of 1–10 and of 20, 30, 40, 50; halves of
  even numbers 2–20 and of 40, 60, 80, 100; ±1, ±2, ±10, ± whole tens; complement to the next ten; add a number
  below 9 bridging the ten (47 + 8 = 47 + 3 + 5); add 9 as +10 −1; add two numbers below 100 by tens and ones; halve
  an even number by splitting (half of 46 = 20 + 3). Fluency with learnt procedures: **9 results in 3 minutes**.
- **Problems**: one-step part-whole and comparison problems, two-step problems, sharing and grouping, numbers to
  100.
- **Money** (« La monnaie », from period 2 or 3): whole euros **up to 100 €**, coins and notes, 10 × 1 € = 10 €,
  make an amount with fewest coins, shop role-play, give change (« rendre la monnaie »). Vocabulary: plus cher,
  moins cher, billet, pièce, somme, reste.
- **Time** (« Le repérage dans le temps »): **o'clock only**: read and set whole hours up to twelve on an analogue
  clock (« trois heures », « midi »), link hours to moments of the day.
- **Length and mass**: compare by direct comparison (long, court; lourd, léger), order masses with a balance. No
  units yet.
- **Shape**: cube, boule, cône, cylindre, pavé; « face »; disque, carré, rectangle, triangle, « sommet », « côté »;
  ruler for alignment and drawing; squared/dotted paper; position and movement vocabulary, class plan, coded
  movement instructions.
- **Data** (« Organisation et gestion de données »): collect data, present it in a table or **bar chart**
  (« diagramme en barres »); double-entry table.
- **Patterns**: ordinal sequences « ABABAB… quelle est la dix-neuvième lettre ? ».
- Tests: évaluations Repères CP in September **and** a « point d'étape » in January.

### CE1 (age 7 to 8)

- **Numbers to 1 000** (hundreds from period 1; to 1 000 by period 2); hundreds, tens, ones; « plaques, barres,
  cubes »; compare and order (234, 243, 239, 300, 229); ordinals to « centième ».
- **Fractions start in CE1** (from period 2): « moitié », « demi », « quart »; unit fractions of a whole then non-
  unit fractions; **only fractions of a whole, ≤ 1, denominators 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 or 10**; compare simple cases
  from period 4 (⅓ > ⅙); relations like three sixths make a half; find the complement to the whole.
- **Addition and subtraction**: column addition of two or three numbers up to three digits (245 + 437); **one
  written subtraction method (« soustraction posée ») by period 3 at the latest**, either « par cassage » or « par
  compensation » (see section 5); **the same method across the school from CE1 to CM2**.
- **Multiplication**: the **× sign, read « fois »**; « 7 × 20 biscuits = 140 biscuits » (units in the calculation
  are encouraged); commutativity (8 × 4 = 4 × 8, salad rows in a vegetable garden).
- **Times tables**: **all the multiplication tables for factors 0 to 10 are learnt over the whole CE1 year**,
  progressively (« L'apprentissage des tables de multiplication s'étale sur l'année scolaire tout entière »); the
  last ones may still be shaky at the end of CE1 and are consolidated in CE2. Target **8 multiplication facts a
  minute** by the end of CE1 (12 addition facts a minute). Also doubles of 1–15, of 20…50 in fives, of 100, 150,
  200, 250, 300, 500; halves of even numbers to 30, of tens to 100, of hundreds to 1 000; 25 × 1, 2, 3, 4.
  The tables stop at **10 × 10** (not 12 × 12).
- **Division**: still through sharing and grouping problems (no ÷ sign yet).
- **Mental maths**: add/subtract whole tens and hundreds; procedures from CP continued; fluency **12 results in 3
  minutes**; all calculations within 1 000.
- **Problems**: bar models appear (« schéma en barre », « schéma avec deux barres »), two-step problems, sharing.
- **Money**: **centimes** introduced (period 2 at the latest), **1 € = 100 centimes**, the **decimal comma for
  money** from period 3 (2 € et 17 centimes = 2,17 €; 2 € et 5 centimes = 2,05 €; 85 centimes = 0,85 €; 17 € =
  17,00 €), compare purses, order prices, give change. No "tenths/hundredths" words yet.
- **Time**: **half past and quarter past/to**, hours above twelve: « trois heures du matin / de l'après-midi »;
  « 2 heures et quart » = « 14 heures et 15 minutes », shown « 14:15 » on a digital clock; units **h** and **min**;
  1 h = 60 min, ½ h = 30 min, ¼ h = 15 min; durations within a day (8 h 30 min to 8 h 45 min; 15 h 45 min to
  16 h 15 min); compare 2 hours and 130 minutes.
- **Length**: **m, cm, km**; 1 m = 100 cm, 1 km = 1 000 m; measure with a ruler or tape; estimate (is a pencil case
  2 cm, 20 cm or 2 m?). No decimals for lengths.
- **Mass**: compare by hefting and with a « balance de type Roberval »; **g and kg are introduced in CE1**
  (« Connaitre et utiliser les unités gramme et kilogramme et les symboles associés (g, kg) », 1 kg = 1 000 g); weigh
  objects in g or kg (Roberval or digital scales); reference masses (a packet of sugar 1 kg, a sachet of baking powder
  about 10 g); order four masses (1 kg et 300 g; 1 000 g; 50 kg; 2 kg et 100 g). No decimal comma for lengths or
  masses (checked against the cycle 2 text, 10 Oct 2026).
- **Shape**: cube, boule, cône, pyramide, cylindre, pavé; « face », « sommet », « arête »; cercle, carré, rectangle,
  triangle, **triangle rectangle**; right angles with the « équerre » and the right-angle code; ruler, set square
  and **compass** (« compas ») as drawing tools.
- **Data**: make and read bar charts and double-entry tables.
- Tests: évaluations Repères CE1 in September.

### CE2 (age 8 to 9)

- **Numbers to 10 000** (by period 2); thousands; compare and order (6 234, 6 243, 6 239, 6 300, 5 229).
- **Fractions**: equalities between fractions (⁵⁄₁₀ = ½); from period 3, **fractions of a unit length on a graduated
  strip (« bande-unité graduée »)**, measuring lengths with fractions, placing fractions in order on the ruler;
  compare fractions with the same denominator, the same numerator, or where one denominator is a multiple of the
  other; **add and subtract fractions** with the same denominator (and where one denominator is a multiple of the
  other); denominators **≤ 12**, all fractions ≤ 1.
- **Addition and subtraction**: words « terme », « somme », « différence »; written methods for whole numbers to
  10 000, and **with decimals in money problems** (4,56 € + 15,30 €; 74,56 € − 15,30 €).
- **Multiplication**: words « facteur », « produit », « multiple »; even numbers are multiples of 2; **written
  multiplication (« multiplication posée ») introduced by period 4 at the latest**: a two- or three-digit number by a
  one- or two-digit number (16 × 548), with the number with fewer digits on the second line.
- **Division**: meaning, **the ÷ sign**, division as the inverse of multiplication (7 × 13 = 91, so 91 ÷ 7 = 13).
  No written division method yet.
- **Mental maths**: addition facts **15 a minute**, multiplication facts **12 a minute**; doubles of 1–20, of 20…75,
  of 100…600; fluency **15 results in 3 minutes**; e.g. × 40 as × 4 × 10.
- **Problems**: one- and two-step, bar models or a number-line model, fraction problems with the same denominator.
- **Money**: decimal comma for money; give change by counting up (change from 5 € for 3,68 €: 32 centimes to 4 €,
  then 1 €).
- **Time**: read the time to the minute, analogue and digital (« huit heures et demie », « dix heures trente-cinq »,
  « sept heures moins le quart », « quatre heures moins vingt », « quinze heures quarante-deux »); set the hands;
  durations within a day; a time line for one- and two-step problems (train leaves at 7 h 10…).
- **Length**: **m, dm, cm, mm, km**; conversions by known relations (no conversion tables in cycle 2): 1 cm = 10 mm,
  215 cm = 2 m 1 dm 5 cm, 5 km + 750 m = 5 750 m; **perimeter** (« périmètre », the length of the outline),
  transfer sides with a compass, measure each side; **no formula** for square and rectangle perimeters.
- **Mass**: **the tonne (t) is added** to g and kg; 1 kg = 1 000 g, 1 t = 1 000 kg; 5 462 g = 5 kg + 462 g;
  5 350 kg = 5 t 350 kg.
- **Capacity** (« contenances »): **L, dL, cL**; 1 L = 10 dL = 100 cL; estimate a glass, bottle, bucket.
- **Shape**: name cube, boule, pavé, cône, pyramide, cylindre; **net of a cube** (« patron »); carré, rectangle,
  triangle, triangle rectangle, **losange** (rhombus); equal-length coding; **lines of symmetry** by folding or
  tracing paper, complete a symmetric figure on squared paper.
- **Data**: tables and bar charts, including completing a double-entry table with totals.
- Tests: évaluations Repères CE2 in September.

### CM1 (age 9 to 10), cycle 3

- **Numbers to 999 999** (but only to four digits in periods 1 and 2; five and six digits from period 3 or 4);
  multiples and divisors; **divisibility rules for 2, 5 and 10 only**.
- **Fractions** (worked on from period 1): fractions **greater than 1**; write ⁷⁄₄ as 1 + ¾ and back; place fractions
  and mixed sums on a number line; compare, add and subtract; **fraction of a quantity** for unit fractions (a third
  of 12 marbles, a quarter of 100 m); denominators **≤ 20** (decimal fractions up to 100).
- **Decimals** (from period 2): introduced **as decimal fractions** (⁷⁄₁₀, ⁸⁄₁₀₀), then the comma as a code:
  35,78 = 35 + ⁷⁄₁₀ + ⁸⁄₁₀₀; **tenths and hundredths only**; number line, compare, order, integer part, round to the
  nearest whole. Money gives decimal problems from period 1.
- **Operations**: estimate; brackets; column addition and subtraction **of decimals**; written multiplication of two
  whole numbers, and of a decimal by a whole number below 10; **written division (« division euclidienne ») with a
  one-digit divisor** (quotient and remainder, laid out « en potence »).
- **Mental maths**: facts with whole numbers, simple fraction relations and decimal equivalents of common fractions
  (½ = 0,5…); × 10, 100, 1 000 for whole numbers, × 10 and ÷ 10 for decimals; ± 8, 9, 18, 19, 28, 29, 38, 39; × 4
  or × 8 (doubling), × 5; distributivity in simple cases.
- **Problems**: at least 10 a week; additive (one to three steps), multiplicative « parties-tout », multiplicative
  comparison, mixed, counting (« dénombrement ») and optimisation problems.
- **Algebra** (new strand): missing-number equalities (« 178 − … = 6 × 8 »), a symbol or letter for an unknown,
  « programme de calcul », number sequences and growing patterns; bar models for unknowns.
- **Proportion** (« proportionnalité », its own part): recognise and solve proportionality problems by reasoning
  (13 € a T-shirt, price of 6?), **without** the « coefficient de proportionnalité » or the « produit en croix »
  (those are not taught in CM).
- **Measures**: length mm to km, mass **mg to kg and t**, capacity **mL to hL**; conversions by known relations, not
  by conversion tables (« 3,5 mètres est égal à 350 centimètres, car 1 mètre est égal à 100 centimètres »);
  perimeter problems; **area introduced** (compare surfaces, count squares, **cm²**), no formulas required;
  **angles** compared (vocabulary and notation), only angles less than 180°; time in hours and minutes, one- and
  two-step duration problems.
- **Geometry**: ruler, set square, compass; perpendicular and parallel lines; circle and disc; triangle (rectangle,
  isocèle, équilatéral), quadrilatère, carré, rectangle, losange with their properties; symmetry on squared paper;
  solids incl. **prisme droit**; nets of a cube; perspective drawings; coded movements.
- **Data and probability**: tables, bar charts, **line graphs (« courbes »)**, points on a grid; first
  **probability**: « impossible », « possible », « certain », « probable », « peu probable », « une chance sur deux »
  (coin, dice, 32-card pack).
- Tests: évaluations Repères CM1 in September.

### CM2 (age 10 to 11)

New programme in force from this rentrée (2026).

- **Numbers to 999 999 999** (millions); six digits only in periods 1 and 2; divisors of numbers to 100, all
  divisors of numbers to 30, common divisors (≤ 30) and common multiples (< 15); divisibility by 2, 5, 10 only.
- **Fractions**: as CM1, plus **whole number × fraction**, and fractions of quantities with any numerator (two
  thirds of 12 €, three quarters of 100 m); denominators **≤ 60** (decimal fractions 100 or 1 000).
- **Decimals**: extended to **thousandths** (« millièmes »); same skills as CM1.
- **Operations**: estimate; one or two pairs of brackets; written multiplication of a decimal by a whole number;
  **written decimal division (« division décimale ») with a one-digit divisor**, dividend whole or decimal. Written
  division with a **two-digit divisor is not in the CM programme**.
- **Mental maths**: half of odd numbers to 15; × and ÷ 10, 100, 1 000 for decimals; add two decimals below 10 with
  one decimal place; ± 8, 9, 18, 19 … 98, 99; e.g. 30 × 400; double and halve decimals; ÷ 4 or ÷ 8; × 5 and × 50
  for decimals; "carry" when adding a whole number to a decimal.
- **Problems**: additive and mixed multi-step, multiplicative, comparison, counting, optimisation, problems
  preparing for algorithms; at least 10 a week.
- **Algebra**: missing numbers, problems with unknowns, run **or write** a « programme de calcul », sequences, the
  number of items at a given step of a growing pattern (e.g. price = (N × 12) + 5).
- **Proportion**: continued, by reasoning only (no coefficient, no cross-multiplication).
- **Measures**: area in **cm², dm², m²**, conversions, **area of a square or a rectangle**; perimeter rules children
  can find themselves (a square is four times its side), no formulas to memorise; angles: build sums and multiples,
  halve by folding, **a right angle is 90°** (the **protractor is not used in CM2**, it comes at collège); time with
  **seconds** (base sixty).
- **Geometry**: adds **trapèze**, trapèze rectangle, **pentagone**, **hexagone**; « programme de construction »;
  symmetry including about a diagonal of the grid; nets of a cube and a pavé.
- **Data and probability**: adds **pie charts (« diagrammes circulaires »)** to read; probability as « a chances
  sur b », independence (the dice "doesn't remember"), outcomes listed in a table or a tree.
- Tests: évaluations Repères CM2 in September; at collège, the 6e assessment in September.

## 5. Written methods (« opérations posées », « techniques opératoires ») and their layout

General: « poser une opération » = set out a written calculation in columns; « calcul en ligne » = working written
on one line; « calcul mental » = in the head. Digits line up: « les unités sous les unités, les dizaines sous les
dizaines… ». Children often write the column letters **c d u** (centaines, dizaines, unités) or **m c d u** above.
Pupils are told to prefer mental calculation whenever it is reasonable.

**Addition posée** (CP P4–5; CE1 onwards): numbers stacked, + on the left, a ruled line, answer below. When a
column makes ten or more, the child writes the units and puts the « retenue » (carry) as a small 1 at the top of
the next column: « 7 + 5 = 12, je pose 2 et je retiens 1 ». Words: « la retenue », « je retiens ».

**Soustraction posée** (CE1, by period 3): the programme names two methods and asks each school to keep **one
method from CE1 to CM2**:

1. **« par cassage »** (decomposition, like breaking a ten): if the top digit is too small, cross out the top tens
   digit, write one less above it and put 10 more with the units (« je casse une dizaine »). Matches the base-ten
   blocks.
2. **« par compensation »** (the traditional French method, "equal additions"): if the top digit is too small, add
   10 to that top digit and add 1 to the bottom number's digit in the next column (« j'ajoute dix unités en haut et
   une dizaine en bas »). On paper: a small 1 next to the top digit, and a small 1 (the « retenue ») under or beside
   the bottom digit in the next column. Spoken: « 3 moins 8, je ne peux pas ; 13 moins 8 égale 5, je retiens 1 ».
   **UNCERTAIN** which method is more common nationally today: compensation is what most parents learnt; cassage
   is widespread in newer textbooks. Pages should show both and tell parents to ask which one the school uses.

**Multiplication posée** (CE2 by period 4; then CM): the number with fewer digits on the second line, × on the left.
For a two-digit multiplier, one line of partial products per digit: first the units line, then the tens line
shifted one place left, with a **0** (some teachers use a dot) written in the units column first; then add the
lines. Carries are written small, often above, and crossed out before the next line. Grid method and "gelosia"
are not the standard French method (some teachers show the grid to explain, **UNCERTAIN**, don't present it as the
school method).

**Division posée « en potence »** (CM1: « division euclidienne », one-digit divisor; CM2: « division décimale »,
one-digit divisor, dividend whole or decimal): the dividend on the left; to its right a vertical line, and a
horizontal line under the divisor, forming an upside-down "T"/gallows (the « potence »). The divisor is written top
right, the **quotient under the horizontal line, below the divisor**. The child takes enough digits of the dividend
(« on prend… »), asks « en 17, combien de fois 3 ? 5 fois », writes 5 in the quotient, writes the product and the
subtraction under the dividend (many teachers now ask for the subtraction to be written; some let pupils do it
mentally), brings down the next digit (« j'abaisse le 4 »), and repeats. The remainder (« le reste ») ends at the
bottom left. Check: dividende = (diviseur × quotient) + reste, and reste < diviseur. Vocabulary: « dividende »,
« diviseur », « quotient », « reste ». In CM2 decimal division, the child places the comma in the quotient when
bringing down the first digit after the comma, and may add zeros to continue.

**Before written methods**: « calcul en ligne » with jumps on a number line (« +3, +5 » arcs), decomposing into
tens and units (47 + 28 = 60 + 15 = 75), the slate (« ardoise ») to note intermediate results. The programme shows
this as the « contenu de l'ardoise ».

## 6. Notation

- **Decimal comma**: 2,5; 0,75; 3,14. Never a point.
- **Thousands separator**: a space (non-breaking): 1 000; 6 234; 10 000; 999 999 999. The programmes use the space
  even for four-digit numbers (6 234). No commas.
- **Multiplication**: **×**, read « fois » (CE1 onwards). The dot is not used in primary.
- **Division**: **÷** (the CE2 programme: « utiliser le symbole ÷ »). The colon « : » also appears in some textbooks
  and older habits (**UNCERTAIN** how often); use ÷ on our pages. The written method is the « potence ».
- **Minus**: use the true minus sign − on pages (the programme PDF uses a hyphen, which is only typography).
- **Fractions**: written as a stacked fraction (¾, ⁵⁄₁₀) and read « trois quarts », « cinq dixièmes »: « un demi »,
  « un tiers », « un quart », then « cinquièmes, sixièmes… » (ordinal names), « dixièmes », « centièmes »,
  « millièmes ». « La moitié » is used for half of an amount. Numerator « numérateur », denominator
  « dénominateur ». « Fraction unitaire » (numerator 1). Equivalent fractions: the programme speaks of « égalités
  entre fractions » / « fractions égales »; « fractions équivalentes » is common in textbooks too.
- **Comparison**: =, <, > (« est égal à », « est inférieur à », « est supérieur à », « est compris entre … et … »).
- **Time**: written **15 h 30** (programme style: « 8 h 30 », durations « 1 h 30 min », « 8 h 30 min »); digital
  displays « 15:30 » (the CE1 text writes « 14 : 15 »). On pages use « 15 h 30 » with non-breaking spaces; « 15h30 »
  is also common informally. Seconds: « s ».
- **Money**: amount, space, then the sign: **2,50 €**; 17,00 €; 0,85 €. Spoken « deux euros cinquante ». Cents
  are « centimes » (« 50 centimes », sometimes written 50 c). Never € before the number.
- **Units**: metric only; m, dm, cm, mm, km; g, kg, t, mg; L, dL, cL, mL, hL (the programme uses capital L); cm²,
  dm², m²; degrees 90°. A non-breaking space between number and unit: 3 km, 250 g, 1,5 L.
- **Percent** (6e and everyday life only): 25 % with a non-breaking space.
- **Ordinals**: 1er / 1re, 2e, 3e (not 2ème in careful writing). Centuries in Roman numerals (« XXIe siècle »);
  Roman numerals are not in the primary maths programme (they appear in 6e as a history-of-numbers topic).
- **Geometry**: segment [AB], line (AB), length AB; the programme says pupils needn't know these codes in CM.
- **Number words**: France uses « soixante-dix », « quatre-vingts », « quatre-vingt-dix » (not « septante »,
  « nonante », which are Belgian/Swiss). The irregular 70–99 names are a well-known stumbling block for place value
  (« soixante-dix » = 7 dizaines): worth a mention on counting pages. The programmes spell number words with the
  1990 rule of hyphens everywhere (« trente-et-un », « dix-mille »). Both spellings are correct; on our pages, when
  showing how children write numbers in letters, follow the programme's hyphenated forms.

French typography for all pages (as on our site and apps): a non-breaking space (U+00A0) before « : ; ! ? » and
inside « guillemets »; the curly apostrophe ’; « œ » ligature (cœur, œuf). Spelling: traditional, as in our apps
(« connaître », « maîtrise », « boîte », « entraîner »); the programme texts use the 1990 rectified spelling
(« connaitre », « maitrise »): keep their spelling only inside direct quotations.
