## 1. The school system

| Stage | Years (local names) | Ages | Compulsory? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kinderopvang, peuterspeelzaal / VVE ("voorschoolse educatie") | "de peuters" | 2 to 4 | No |
| **Basisschool** (also "het basisonderwijs", "de lagere school" in older speech) | **groep 1 to groep 8**. Groep 1 and 2 are "de kleuters" / "de kleutergroepen" (often one mixed class "groep 1/2"); groep 3 to 8 are often split into "onderbouw", "middenbouw" and "bovenbouw" (boundaries vary by school) | 4 to 12 | From the first day of the month after the 5th birthday (see below) |
| Voortgezet onderwijs ("de middelbare school") | "de brugklas" (first year), then vmbo (4 years), havo (5), vwo (6), or praktijkonderwijs | 12 to 16/18 | Yes, leerplicht to 16, then kwalificatieplicht to 18 |

- **Children may start school the day after their 4th birthday** (most do), and enter groep 1 whenever that is in
  the year: there is **no single intake date**. Before that, 3-year-olds often have a few "wenmomenten" (settling-in
  mornings). Leerplicht starts **"op de eerste dag van de maand nadat het kind 5 jaar is geworden"** (Rijksoverheid).
  A 4-year-old on the roll is not yet leerplichtig.
- Moving from groep 2 to groep 3 depends on the child's development, not a fixed birthday; many schools use about
  1 October as a guide, and children born in October to December ("oktober-/najaarskinderen", "herfstkinderen")
  may spend a shorter or longer time in the kleutergroepen ("kleuterverlenging"). **UNCERTAIN** (school policy
  varies): pages should say "meestal" and not give a rule.
- So a groep 3 class is mostly children who turn 7 during that school year: "groep 3 (6 tot 7 jaar)". Dutch
  children in groep N are the same age as English children in Year N−1 (groep 3 ≈ Year 2 by age).
- Writing years: **"groep 3"** (lowercase "groep" in running text, number in figures), "in groep 5", "groep 7 en 8",
  "de bovenbouw". Children say "ik zit in groep 4". Kleuters: "groep 1 en 2", "de kleuterklas".
- Teachers: children say **"juf"** (female) and **"meester"** (male), often with the first name ("juf Sanne",
  "meester Ruben"); formal word "de leerkracht" (or "leraar/lerares"). Other school people: "de intern begeleider"
  ("de IB'er", in charge of extra support), "de directeur", "remedial teacher" (RT), "de plusklas" (extra challenge
  for quick learners).
- The ministry (OCW) sets **kerndoelen** (what schools must offer by the end of primary) and the
  **referentieniveaus** (what children should master at the end of primary). Schools choose their own
  **rekenmethode** (textbook series, often with a digital platform) and decide how to spread the goals over the
  years. So per-groep content on our pages is "what the SLO goals and most methods do", and pages should say once
  that "het precieze moment verschilt per school en per rekenmethode".
- The subject is called **"rekenen"** (also "rekenen-wiskunde" in official texts). Children "hebben rekenen", do
  "sommen" in their "rekenschrift" or on a tablet. "Wiskunde" is the secondary-school word.

### Years our Learn section covers

The English section covers ages 4/5 to 11 (end of pre-school to end of primary). The Dutch one covers **the whole
basisschool: groep 1 and 2 (one page for the kleuters) and groep 3 to 8**, ages 4 to 12. Our app store copy already
says "van groep 2 tot groep 8" (ages 5 to 12) for Beequation Kids: pages can say the app suits children from groep 2.

### School calendar and reports

- School year 1 August to 31 July (Leerplichtwet). Summer holidays (6 weeks) are staggered over **three regions:
  Noord, Midden and Zuid**. 2026-2027 (OCW advice dates): herfstvakantie 10–18 Oct (Noord) or 17–25 Oct (Midden,
  Zuid); kerstvakantie 19 Dec 2026–3 Jan 2027; voorjaarsvakantie 20–28 Feb (Noord, Midden) or 13–21 Feb (Zuid, around
  carnaval); meivakantie 24 Apr–2 May 2027 (around Koningsdag, 27 April); zomervakantie from 10, 17 or 24 July
  depending on region. So school starts again in late August or early September. Pages: say "na de zomervakantie",
  not dates.
- Schools set their own school day: many have a "continurooster" (e.g. 8.30–14.15, lunch in class), others have a
  long lunch break and a free Wednesday afternoon. **UNCERTAIN** in detail; use only as colour.
- Reports: the "rapport" usually comes two or three times a year (often February and June/July), with an
  "oudergesprek" or "tienminutengesprek" (and often a "kindgesprek"). Form is the school's choice (words such as
  "goed / voldoende / onvoldoende", or marks in the bovenbouw, plus LVS results). **UNCERTAIN** (varies by school).
- Repeating a year ("blijven zitten", "doubleren") is possible but the school's decision and not common; extending
  the kleuter period ("kleuterverlenging") is a separate case. Phrase loosely.

## 2. What the curriculum expects, year by year

**How the official curriculum is built.**

1. **Kerndoelen** (what schools must offer by the end of primary). The **new kerndoelen (2026)** for rekenen en
   wiskunde in primary school are nine short goals, kerndoel 10 to 18: "De leerling redeneert en rekent met getallen
   en verhoudingen" (10); "toont inzicht bij het handelen met grootheden en eenheden" (11); "interpreteert data"
   (12); "toont inzicht in patronen en verbanden" (13); "toont inzicht bij meetkundig handelen" (14); "gebruikt
   wiskundige denk-werkwijzen" (15); "gebruikt wiskundetaal en wiskundig gereedschap" (16); "ontwikkelt een
   wiskundige attitude" (17); "past wiskunde toe in bekende en nieuwe situaties" (18). They came into force on
   1 August 2026, but schools may keep working with the old ones until 1 August 2031, and a CvTE/SLO/Cito consortium
   is still writing the detailed specification (planned for primary by the end of 2027). The **old kerndoelen
   (2006, nos. 23–33)** are what most current rekenmethodes follow; two are worth quoting:
   - kerndoel 27: "De leerlingen leren de basisbewerkingen met gehele getallen in elk geval tot 100 snel uit het
     hoofd uitvoeren, waarbij optellen en aftrekken tot 20 en de tafels van buiten gekend zijn."
   - kerndoel 30: "De leerlingen leren schriftelijk optellen, aftrekken, vermenigvuldigen en delen volgens meer of
     minder verkorte standaardprocedures."
   Pages: mention kerndoelen briefly ("de kerndoelen: wat elke basisschool moet aanbieden"); say "nieuwe kerndoelen
   sinds 2026" only on teachers' pages, without claiming schools have switched.
2. **Referentieniveaus** (what children should master at the end of groep 8): **1F** (fundamenteel: the basic level
   almost every child should reach) and **1S** (streefniveau: the higher target that many children reach; for taal
   the higher level is called 2F). Four domains: **Getallen, Verhoudingen, Meten en meetkunde, Verbanden**. Every
   doorstroomtoets must report them.
3. **SLO tussendoelen (2017)**: goals per groep, "eind groep 2" to "eind groep 8", building up to 1S, in the domains
   Getallen (getalbegrip, bewerkingen), Verhoudingen (incl. breuken and procenten), Meten & meetkunde, Verbanden.
   Each groep says "beheerst de doelen van groep 2 t/m N−1, ook op het niveau van groep N". Below is what each groep
   adds, with short quotes. Methods can be a little earlier or later.

Note: the SLO numbers below are "ten minste" (at least): e.g. "getallen tot ten minste 100" in groep 4.

### Groep 1 en 2 (4 tot 6 jaar), goals for eind groep 2

- Numbers: say the counting sequence "tot ten minste 20"; count on from any number to 20 and back from below 10;
  ordinal words to 10 ("eerste, tweede, derde"); estimate, count and show amounts to at least 12; compare amounts to
  20 ("meer, minder, evenveel, meeste, minste"); recognise small amounts **to 6 without counting** (dice patterns,
  fingers: "structuren"); read the symbols 0 to 10 and link symbol, word and amount to 10.
- Operations: solve simple adding and taking-away problems with real objects "met hoeveelheden tot ten minste 12
  (handelend)"; simple sharing ("verdeelsituaties") to 12.
- Ratio: compare sizes in context ("hoe groter je bent, des te groter de kleding").
- Measurement: words for length, size, volume, weight (lang/kort, groot/klein, vol/leeg, zwaar/licht) and comparing;
  measuring with hands or feet ("natuurlijke maat"), a cup or a balance; time words (dag, nacht, vandaag, morgen,
  straks, eerder, later) and putting events in order.
- Money: knows things cost money; **pays exact amounts under 10 euro with 1-euro coins** and totals up to ten 1-euro
  coins.
- Shape and space: voor, achter, naast, in, op, boven, onder; links, rechts, tussen, tegenover; following a route;
  copying a building; **patterns** (kralenplank, rijgen, mozaïek); naming "cirkel, driehoek, vierkant, rechthoek".
- Data: comparing amounts by laying objects in rows (a first "beelddiagram").
- No test. Schools choose how to follow kleuters (observation systems; an LVS test is not required in groep 1–2).

### Groep 3 (6 tot 7 jaar)

- Numbers: counting **to at least 100**, forwards and backwards; reading numbers to 100; comparing and placing
  numbers **to 20 on a getallenlijn**; seeing amounts to 20 quickly using structures ("handen, turven, vijfstructuur").
- Addition and subtraction: the signs **+, −, =**; **"kent de optellingen, aftrekkingen en splitsingen tot en met 10
  uit het hoofd"**; adding and subtracting **to 20** with strategies: swapping (3 + 6 = 6 + 3), "bijna dubbel"
  (7 + 8 = 7 + 7 + 1), "via de 5", "via de 10" (6 + 8 = 6 + 4 + 4; 12 − 5 = 12 − 2 − 3), analogy (7 − 5 = 2, so
  17 − 5 = 12), the inverse (12 − 6 = 6 because 6 + 6 = 12); explaining how.
- Multiplication and division: informal only: counting in rows or groups or jumps under 20; sharing under 20.
- Measurement: measuring with a natural measure and why we need a fixed one; comparing with a balance.
- Time: days of the week in order; **whole hours on an analogue and a digital clock ("3 uur, 18.00")**; linking hours
  to the day ("om 7 uur sta je op").
- Money: **coins of 1 and 2 euro and notes of 5 and 10 euro**; making amounts **to 20 euro**.
- Shape: links, rechts, tegenover, tussen; views from above; following a plan of the school; folding; nets of simple
  3D shapes ("uitslagen").
- Data: reading a simple **beelddiagram** and **staafdiagram**, making a bar chart by colouring squares; continuing
  patterns.
- Usual practice (UNCERTAIN, method-dependent): the "rekenrek" (20 beads) and "splitshuisjes"; sums to 20 "over het
  tiental" in the second half of the year.

### Groep 4 (7 tot 8 jaar)

- Numbers **to at least 100**: write, split into "tientallen en eenheden", "positiewaarde", count in jumps of 2, 5
  and 10, place on a getallenlijn, **even / oneven**.
- Addition and subtraction: **"kent de optellingen en aftrekkingen tot en met 20 uit het hoofd"**; adding and
  subtracting **to 100** with the standard strategies **"rijgen"** (64 − 27: 64 − 20 = 44, 44 − 7 = 37) and
  **"splitsen"** (34 + 27: 30 + 20 = 50, 4 + 7 = 11, 50 + 11 = 61), plus "via het tiental" (37 + 8 = 37 + 3 + 5),
  "compenseren" (67 − 19 = 67 − 20 + 1), "aanvullen / verschil bepalen" (50 − 48), analogy (80 − 30), estimating.
- Multiplication: meaning ("aantal keer": groups, rows, jumps); the sign **x** ("keer", "maal"); **"kent de
  producten uit de tafels van 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 en 10 uit het hoofd"**; strategies: repeated addition, swapping,
  "één keer meer / minder" (9 x 4 = 10 x 4 − 4), doubling (6 x 7 is double 3 x 7) and halving (5 x 7 is half of
  10 x 7). Division: informal sharing under 100, also with a remainder.
- Ratio: "de helft" of a whole and of an amount; first **verhoudingstabel** (pre-structured, e.g. a recipe).
- Measurement: **metre and centimetre** (1 m = 100 cm), ruler and tape measure; **kilogram**; perimeter by measuring
  or counting squares.
- Time: **"uur, kwartier, half uur, maand, week, dag, seizoen"**; **halve uren en kwartieren** on analogue and digital
  clocks, converting between them ("'kwart over 3' en 15.15"); calculating with whole and half hours; 12 months and
  4 seasons; reading a calendar.
- Money: **all coins and notes**; amounts to 2 euro with coins; whole amounts to 100 euro with coins and notes;
  changing money ("4 briefjes van 5 voor 1 briefje van 20").
- Shape: front/side/top views; nets of kubus, balk, piramide; naming **kubus, balk, bol, cilinder**; drawing a mirror
  image.
- Data: reading and using a simple table, beelddiagram and staafdiagram; **turven** (tally marks).

### Groep 5 (8 tot 9 jaar)

- Numbers **to at least 1000**: honderdtallen, tientallen, eenheden; positiewaarde; **rounding to hundreds** (879 →
  900); comparing money amounts with two decimals ("€ 7,05 is meer dan € 6,95").
- Addition and subtraction **to 1000**, mentally with round numbers (350 + 200, by analogy with 35 + 20) and on paper
  with **"kolomsgewijs rekenen en cijferen"** (both named), plus estimating; adding simple amounts on a receipt.
- Multiplication and division: the sign **":"** ("delen door", "gedeeld door"); remainder ("rest": 72 in groups of 5
  is 14 rest 2); **"kent alle producten uit de tafels tot en met 10 uit het hoofd"** and works out the "deeltafels"
  quickly; multiplying and dividing with multiples of 10 (6 x 80, 500 : 5); the **"verdeelstrategie"**
  (8 x 36 = 8 x 30 + 8 x 6); doubling and halving (12 x 50 = 6 x 100); estimating (8 x 39 ≈ 8 x 40).
- Ratio and fractions: **"een kwart"** of a whole and of an amount (a quarter of 20 children); verhoudingstabel used
  with insight (recipes).
- Measurement: **mm, dm, km** (1 m = 10 dm, 1 dm = 10 cm, 1 cm = 10 mm, 1 km = 1000 m) and converting; perimeter of
  squares and rectangles; **area by counting squares**; **litre and millilitre** (1 l = 1000 ml); **gram**
  (1000 g = 1 kg); price per kg or litre; speed in km per hour.
- Time: **to the minute** on analogue and digital clocks, converting; "etmaal" = 24 hours; 60 minutes, 15 minutes in
  a "kwartier", 60 seconds; **durations** ("De film begint om 15.15 uur en is om 17.00 uur afgelopen"); calendar
  questions.
- Money: writing **"€ 4,95"**; **change from a note up to 100 euro**.
- Shape: **symmetrie, spiegelas**; tangram; designing a net (a dice).
- Data: tables, beeld- and staafdiagrammen, **legenda**; number patterns (100 − 93 − 86 − 79 …).

### Groep 6 (9 tot 10 jaar)

- Numbers **to 100.000**; "grote hele getallen zowel met een punt als met een spatie" (46.389 and 46 389); rounding
  to hundreds and thousands.
- **Decimals ("kommagetallen") with one and two decimals**: read, write, compare, number line, split into helen,
  tienden, honderdsten (3,25 = 3 + 0,2 + 0,05).
- **Fractions as numbers**: written "met een horizontale streep en met een schuine streep"; **teller and noemer**;
  "stambreuken" (1/4), "niet-stambreuken" (4/5), "hele breuken" (5/5), **"samengestelde breuken"** (2 1/3, i.e. mixed
  numbers); comparing (3/4 liter is more than 1/2 liter); on a number line; making up to 1 (2/7 + … = 1); **fraction
  of an amount** ("1/5 deel van 60 euro; 3/4 deel van 120 liter"); fraction as part of a whole or of an amount.
- Addition and subtraction **under 10.000** (rijgen, splitsen, kolomsgewijs and/or cijferen); money sums.
- Multiplication and division: **"kent de delingen uit de deeltafels tot en met 10 uit het hoofd"**; on paper
  "eencijferige getallen vermenigvuldigen met twee- of driecijferige getallen" (7 dozen met 248 sinaasappels) and
  "twee- of driecijferige getallen delen door een eencijferig getal (ook met rest)" (361 kinderen in bootjes voor 7);
  money × whole number (3 tandenborstels van € 2,25); **"gemiddelde"** in simple cases.
- Ratio: verhoudingstabel; 4 : 5 and 4/5; "1 op de 3"; price per piece, per kg, per litre; speed.
- Measurement: **hm**; decimal notation for measures (2,37 m = 2 m 37 cm; 4,5 km = 4 km 500 m); **perimeter of a
  rectangle 2 x (l + b)**; **m², dm², cm²** and **area = l x b**; **dl, cl** (1 l = 10 dl = 100 cl); kg ↔ g;
  thermometer to tenths of a degree.
- Time: hours ↔ minutes ↔ seconds; stopwatch; kwartaal, schrikkeljaar, decennium, eeuw; timeline.
- Money: amounts up to about 5000 euro with notes and coins, also with as few as possible.
- Shape: scale drawings in words; map legend; **grid references** ("De Domtoren ligt in vak C5"); **windrichtingen**
  (noord, oost, zuid, west); designing symmetric patterns and tilings ("vlakvulling"); names "driehoek, vierhoek,
  vijfhoek, **zeshoek**, cirkel, kubus, balk, bol, piramide, cilinder".
- Data: beelddiagram with one picture = 100; **cirkeldiagram** and **lijngrafiek** (reading); patterns.

### Groep 7 (10 tot 11 jaar)

- Numbers **to 1 miljoen**; "een ton" (100.000), "een half miljoen"; rounding to a sensible precision (371.389 →
  370.000).
- Decimals **to three decimals** (6,175 = 6 + 0,1 + 0,07 + 0,005); why 4,3 > 4,25; rounding; adding and subtracting
  decimals (mentally: 1 − 0,25; on paper: kolomsgewijs or cijferen, "komma's recht onder elkaar"); simple × and ÷
  with decimals (4 x 2,25; 12 x 2,5; 10 : 2,5).
- Fractions: forms of a fraction; **fraction ↔ decimal** (1/5 = 0,2); **vereenvoudigen** and **"helen eruit halen"**
  (17/3 = 5 2/3; 9/12 = 3/4); **gelijkwaardige breuken** (2/5 = 4/10 = 8/20); **adding and subtracting "gelijknamige
  breuken"** (2/7 + 6/7 = 8/7 = 1 1/7); comparing 1/3 and 1/5; fraction as operator (4/5 van 350); "2 : 3 is 2/3";
  whole number divided by a fraction in context (5 liter soep in bakjes van 1/4 liter).
- Operations on paper: "eencijferige getallen vermenigvuldigen met drie- en viercijferige getallen en tweecijferige
  getallen keer tweecijferige getallen" (35 x 67); **"driecijferige getallen delen door een tweecijferig getal"**
  (183 : 58); multiplying and dividing by 10 and 100 (80 x 900; 21000 : 700); gemiddelde.
- **Rekenmachine**: the keys, simple calculations, interpreting a remainder on the screen, checking by estimating.
- Ratio and **percentages to 100%**: reading percentages (korting, cirkeldiagram); **"25% korting"** on € 80;
  breuken with denominators 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50, 100 related to percentages; **scale "1 : 100"**; enlarging a
  photo; price per kg (800 gram kaas voor € 7,50).
- Measurement: **dam**; the prefixes milli-, centi-, deci-, deca-, hecto-, kilo- in order; **km², hm², dam², m², dm²,
  cm², mm², hectare, are** (1 km² = 100 ha = 1.000.000 m²); **area of triangles** and L-shapes; **m³, dm³, cm³**
  (1 dm³ = 1 liter), "kuub"; **volume = l x b x h**; 0,358 kg = 358 g; 1 ton = 1000 kg; **temperature below zero**
  (−4 is colder than −2).
- Time: durations in h, min, s; hundredths of a second in sport; **date notation "10-02-2017"** (dd-mm-jjjj); days
  between two dates.
- Shape: horizontaal, verticaal, diagonaal; perspective; where a photographer stood ("kijklijnen").
- Data: axes words ("horizontale as, verticale as, x-as, y-as, legenda, stijgen, dalen"); drawing a line graph;
  **coordinates** with positive numbers; filling in a pie chart split in ten.

### Groep 8 (11 tot 12 jaar)

- Numbers: **miljoen, miljard**; "1.425.000 is ruim 1,4 miljoen"; rounding decimals to hundredths, tenths, whole
  numbers; **deelbaarheid** (divisibility).
- All four operations with whole numbers and decimals, **choosing an efficient strategy** and explaining it;
  multiplying and dividing decimals (7 meter stof van € 15,75; three-digit by two-digit division with a decimal
  answer); **"de geldende regels voor de volgorde waarin rekenbewerkingen moeten worden uitgevoerd"** incl. brackets
  (4 + 5 x 6 = 34); calculator for multi-step problems, knowing when to use it.
- Fractions: **"ongelijknamige breuken optellen en aftrekken… via de standaardprocedure 'gelijknamig maken'"**;
  whole number × fraction (6 x 3/5); **fraction × fraction** (3/4 x 5/8; 1/4 deel van 1/2 liter); dividing by a
  fraction or mixed number in context (1 1/2 : 1/4).
- Percentages **above 100%** (rente, btw, winst, verlies, groei); calculating a discount percentage (from € 150 to
  € 105 is 30% korting); the original price (€ 30 after 50% korting was € 60); "repeterende breuken" rounded;
  scale 1 : 50.000 on a map.
- Measurement: the whole metric system for length, area, volume and weight (kg, hg, dag, g, dg, cg, mg); area of the
  faces of a cube or cuboid; enlargement and area/volume; negative temperatures on a number line (−5 + 6 = 1).
- Time: time zones; zomertijd/wintertijd; converting eeuw to seconde. Money: **wisselkoersen**. Population density.
- Shape: the **windroos** (N, NO, O, ZO, Z, ZW, W, NW); sun and shadows; a maquette.
- Data: choosing the best chart; trends and conclusions; critical reading of graphs.
- Tests: the **doorstroomtoets** (January/February) and the move to the middelbare school (section 3).

## 4. Written methods and how they are laid out

Dutch primary maths is strongly shaped by **"realistisch rekenen"** (realistic mathematics education, from the
Freudenthal Instituut): contexts first, models such as the getallenlijn and the verhoudingstabel, mental strategies
before written ones, and flexible "kolomsgewijs" methods before (or instead of) the compact algorithms ("cijferen").
SLO groep 5–7 goals name both: **"kolomsgewijs rekenen en/of cijferen"**. Which one a school uses depends on its
**rekenmethode** (examples: De wereld in getallen, Pluspunt, Getal & Ruimte Junior, Alles telt, Reken zeker, Semsom;
digital platforms such as Snappet or Gynzy). Don't recommend one; don't name competitors' apps. Pages should explain
both and tell parents to ask the juf or meester which one the class uses.

Layout convention: numbers under each other, units under units, a line under them, and **the operation sign on the
right of the bottom number** (most Dutch methods; UNCERTAIN for every method, but it is the classic Dutch layout):

### Kolomsgewijs optellen (left to right, whole values)

```
    4 5 6
    3 7 8 +
    -----
    7 0 0     (400 + 300)
    1 2 0     (50 + 70)
      1 4     (6 + 8)
    ----- +
    8 3 4
```

### Cijferend optellen ("onthouden")

```
    1 1          ← "1 onthouden", written small above
    4 5 6
    3 7 8 +
    -----
    8 3 4
```
Said: "6 plus 8 is 14, 4 opschrijven, 1 onthouden. 5 plus 7 is 12, plus 1 is 13, 3 opschrijven, 1 onthouden.
4 plus 3 is 7, plus 1 is 8." Terms: "optellen", "de som", "het totaal", "erbij".

### Kolomsgewijs aftrekken (negative part answers, or "tekort")

```
    8 3 4
    5 4 8 −
    -----
    3 0 0     (800 − 500)
    − 1 0     (30 − 40)
      − 4     (4 − 8)
    -----
    2 8 6
```
Some methods avoid negatives by writing the shortfall as a "tekort"; check the class. Check: 286 + 548 = 834.

### Cijferend aftrekken ("lenen" / "inwisselen")

```
    8 3 4        the 3 tens become 2 (crossed out), the 4 becomes 14;
    5 4 8 −      then the 8 hundreds become 7, the 2 tens become 12
    -----
    2 8 6
```
"4 min 8 kan niet: ik leen 1 tiental (inwisselen), 14 min 8 is 6. 2 min 4 kan niet: ik leen 1 honderdtal, 12 min 4
is 8. 7 min 5 is 2." Terms: "aftrekken", "het verschil", "eraf", "lenen" (parents' word), "inwisselen" (newer).

### Vermenigvuldigen

Kolomsgewijs (partial products, also on paper as "onder elkaar"):
```
      4 8
      3 6 x
    -----
    1 2 0 0     (30 x 40)
      2 4 0     (30 x 8)
      2 4 0     (6 x 40)
        4 8     (6 x 8)
    ----- +
    1 7 2 8
```
Cijferend (compact): 48 x 6 = 288; 48 x 30 = 1440 (written with a 0); 288 + 1440 = 1728. Terms: "keer", "maal",
"het product". The verdeelstrategie on one line: 8 x 36 = 8 x 30 + 8 x 6 = 240 + 48 = 288.

### Delen: "de hapmethode" (happend delen) and "de staartdeling"

Both use the Dutch frame **"deler / deeltal \ uitkomst"**. The **hapmethode** takes easy "happen" (bites: 10x,
20x, 100x the divisor) and adds them up; many schools now teach it instead of the classic staartdeling, or before it:
```
    7 / 875 \
        700 −     100 x
        ---
        175
        140 −      20 x
        ---
         35
         35 −       5 x
         --
          0       125 x     → 875 : 7 = 125
```
The **staartdeling** (classic long division), digit by digit (Wikipedia NL example, checked):
```
    11 / 135 \ 12
         11
         --
          25
          22
          --
           3      → 135 : 11 = 12 rest 3   (check: 12 x 11 + 3 = 135)
```
Terms: "delen", "gedeeld door", "het deeltal", "de deler", "de uitkomst" (or "het quotiënt"), "de rest", "het gaat
(niet) precies". Children check with "terugrekenen" (× to check ÷). England's "bus stop" and grid method are not
used; don't show them.

### Mental strategies (the core of Dutch maths)

- **rijgen** (keep the first number whole, add or subtract in jumps on the getallenlijn: 64 − 27 = 64 − 20 − 7),
- **splitsen** (split both numbers into tens and ones: 34 + 27 = 30 + 20 + 4 + 7),
- **via het tiental** / "over het tiental" (8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3),
- **compenseren** (67 − 19 = 67 − 20 + 1), **aanvullen / verschil bepalen** (50 − 48: count up),
- **verwisselen**, **(bijna) dubbel**, **verdubbelen en halveren** (18 x 25 = 9 x 50), **verdeelstrategie**,
- **schattend rekenen** (estimate first), and the **verhoudingstabel** for ratio, percentages and division.

## 5. Notation

- **Decimal comma**: 2,5 ("twee komma vijf"); children's word "kommagetallen", formal "decimale getallen";
  "tienden, honderdsten, duizendsten". Never a point for decimals.
- **Thousands**: SLO: "grote hele getallen zowel met een punt als met een spatie" (46.389 and 46 389); SLO writes
  4-digit numbers without a separator (1000, 3800, 7500) and 10.000, 100.000, 1.000.000 with points. **Rule for our
  pages: no separator in 4-digit numbers (1000, 2026), a point from 10.000 upwards (10.000, 250.000); never in
  years or times.**
- **Multiplication**: **x** / **×** ("keer", "maal"). SLO: "het vermenigvuldigteken x (keer, maal, vermenigvuldigen
  met)". Use the "×" character on pages (it looks like the x children write). Not "·", not "*".
- **Division**: **":"** ("gedeeld door", "delen door"): SLO: "het deelteken ':'". Write 24 : 6 = 4 with spaces. Not
  "÷" (only on calculators): don't use ÷ on Dutch pages.
- **Minus**: "−", read "min". **Equals**: "=", "is" (3 + 4 is 7). "<" "kleiner dan", ">" "groter dan".
- **Fractions**: stacked, or with a slash ("met een horizontale streep en met een schuine streep", SLO): ½, ¾, 3/4.
  Read "een half / de helft", "een derde", "een kwart" or "een vierde", "drie kwart" or "drie vierde", "twee derde",
  "een vijfde", "een zesde", "een achtste", "een tiende", "een twaalfde". Mixed numbers ("samengestelde breuken",
  also "gemengde getallen"): "2 ½", "twee en een half"; "anderhalf" = 1 ½. "Helen eruit halen" = turning 8/7 into
  1 1/7.
- **Percent**: "25%" (no space, as SLO and most Dutch texts write it), read "vijfentwintig procent".
- **Ordinals**: 1e, 2e, 3e ("eerste, tweede, derde").
- **Units**: metric only, symbols without full stops: mm, cm, dm, m, dam, hm, km; g, kg, ton; ml, cl, dl, l (also L);
  m², m³; "uur", "min", "sec" (or s). Hectare (ha), are (a). The "kuub" = m³.
- **Money**: **"€ 4,95"** (euro sign before the amount, usually with a space; SLO also writes €4,95); round amounts
  "€ 80,-" or "€ 80" (the ",-" is common on price tags). Read "vier euro vijfennegentig". Cents: "45 cent" or "€ 0,45".
- **Times**: in running text **"15.15 uur"** (point, as SLO and Dutch style write it); a digital clock display may
  show 15:15. 24-hour times are normal in writing (timetables, TV, school).
- **Dates**: "10 oktober 2026", or "10-10-2026" (dd-mm-jjjj, SLO groep 7). The week starts on **maandag**.
- **Number words**: Dutch says the units before the tens: 21 = "eenentwintig" (one-and-twenty), 47 =
  "zevenenveertig". This "inversie" makes children write 12 for 21 or 74 for 47: a classic Dutch difficulty worth a
  tip on the counting page. Numbers are written as one word up to "duizend" ("tweehonderdvijfendertig").
- **Large numbers**: miljoen = 10⁶, **miljard = 10⁹**, biljoen = 10¹² (not the English billion). "Een ton" = 100.000
  (also 1000 kg: context).

## 9. Form of address and tone

- **"Je/jij"** for parents and teachers alike, as in the apps ("Indie de bij moedigt je kind aan"), the store copy
  ("Children and parents are both addressed with 'je/jij'") and i18n/nl.json ("Speel gratis in je browser", "Gratis
  puzzels voor je klas"). **Never "u".**
- Parents: "**je kind**" (gender-neutral; refer back with "het" or "hij of zij" sparingly; "je zoon of dochter" now
  and then). "Ouders" is the usual word ("Voor ouders", "Ouderhoek"); "ouders en verzorgers" once on hub pages.
  Teachers: "**leerkrachten**" ("Voor leerkrachten"), "je klas", "je leerlingen", "de groep".
- Warm, direct, plain: a Dutch juf talking to a parent at the "schoolplein". Dutch style is matter-of-fact: avoid
  hype, avoid "simpel", "makkelijk" or "gewoon even" (it isn't easy for everyone). No emoji. Use the words children
  hear in class, then explain them ("wat de juf 'rijgen' noemt…").
- Spelling: Groene Boekje (e.g. "kommagetal", "staafdiagram", "groep 8", "tafeltjes"); quotes "…" or '…' (nl.json uses
  ‘…’ in places: pick one style per page).
