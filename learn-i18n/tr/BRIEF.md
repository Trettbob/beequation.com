## 1. The school system

Turkey has **12 years of compulsory schooling in three stages of four years ("4+4+4")**: ilkokul, ortaokul, lise.
Pre-school is not compulsory.

| Stage | Years (local names) | Ages | Compulsory? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Okul öncesi eğitim: "anaokulu" (separate nursery school, 36-68 months) or "ana sınıfı" / "anasınıfı" (a class inside a primary school, 57-68 months at the end of September) | groups by age: "3 yaş", "4 yaş", "5 yaş" (in plans: "36-48 ay", "48-60 ay", "60-72 ay") | 3 to 6 | **No** (optional; MEB's stated aim is one year of pre-school for every child) |
| İlkokul (primary school) | **1., 2., 3., 4. sınıf** | about 6 to 10 | Yes |
| Ortaokul (middle school; also "imam hatip ortaokulu") | **5., 6., 7., 8. sınıf** | about 10 to 14 | Yes |
| Lise (high school) | 9. to 12. sınıf (some with a "hazırlık" year) | about 14 to 18 | Yes |

- **Starting age** (Yönetmelik art. 11): children who are **69 months old by the end of September** are enrolled in
  1. sınıf; **66, 67 and 68-month-olds may start if a parent asks in writing**; 69-71-month-olds can be held back a
  year at a parent's written request (then usually sent to pre-school). So a 1. sınıf class is about 5 y 6 m to
  6 y 9 m in September, and the year is described as "6-7 yaş". Enrolment is automatic from the MERNİS address
  (no application) (press, UNCERTAIN in detail).
- **Ana sınıfı** takes children who are 57-68 months at the end of September (Yönetmelik art. 4 and 11); since July 2026,
  in districts without a state anaokulu, ana sınıfları may take 36-68-month-olds.
- The cut-off is **30 September**, close to England's 1 September: a Turkish "N. sınıf" child is about one year older
  than the English Year with the same number (1. sınıf ≈ English Year 2 by age).
- Writing the years: "1. sınıf" (an ordinal number with a full stop; "sınıf" lower case in running text, "1. Sınıf"
  in titles and on book covers), read "birinci sınıf"; "ilkokul 3. sınıf", "ortaokul 5. sınıf". The 5-year-old group is
  "anasınıfı" (TDK spelling, one word, as parents write it) or "ana sınıfı" (the regulation's spelling): use
  **"anasınıfı"** on our pages, and "5 yaş grubu" / "okul öncesi" where it helps.
- Teachers: in ilkokul one **"sınıf öğretmeni"** teaches almost every subject, and usually **keeps the same class from
  1. to 4. sınıf** (common practice, UNCERTAIN as a rule). From 5. sınıf subjects are taught by **"branş öğretmeni"**
  (here "matematik öğretmeni"). Pre-school teacher: "okul öncesi öğretmeni" ("öğretmen"). Parents call the teacher
  "öğretmenim" or "X Öğretmen" / "X Hanım", "X Bey".
- Schools: "devlet okulu" (state school), "özel okul" (private school); "okul müdürü"; "rehber öğretmen" /
  "psikolojik danışman" (school counsellor); "RAM" (Rehberlik ve Araştırma Merkezi: assessment for special needs).
- Curriculum is **national**, set by the Talim ve Terbiye Kurulu (TTKB) of MEB. MEB provides **free textbooks**
  ("ders kitabı", also an "etkinlik kitabı" in some subjects) and EBA (Eğitim Bilişim Ağı) for digital content.
  Many families also buy private practice and test books ("yardımcı kaynak", "soru bankası", "deneme"); don't name
  publishers.
- **Weekly maths time**: 5 lessons ("ders saati") a week in every grade 1 to 8 (TYMM ilkokul and ortaokul programmes).

### The curriculum change in progress ("Türkiye Yüzyılı Maarif Modeli", TYMM)

New programmes were published in 2024 and are phased in year by year. In **2026-2027**: "1, 2, 3 ve 5, 6, 7.
sınıflarda Türkiye Yüzyılı Maarif Modeli Öğretim Programları uygulanacaktır" (tymm.meb.gov.tr). **4. sınıf still
follows the 2018 programme in 2026-2027**; it is expected to switch in 2027-2028 (UNCERTAIN, not on an official
page). Pre-school uses the TYMM pre-school programme. On the 4. sınıf page, describe what 4. sınıf learns now (2018)
and say in one line that a new programme is on the way; on topic tables use content that is true in both.

TYMM vocabulary teachers will know (parents won't): "öğrenme çıktısı" (learning outcome; replaces "kazanım"),
"tema" (unit), "alan becerileri" (subject skills), "beceri temelli", "farklılaştırma" with "zenginleştirme"
(extension) and "destekleme" (support), "öğrenme kanıtları" (assessment evidence), "performans görevi",
"Erdem-Değer-Eylem Çerçevesi" (values). The five maths "alan becerileri": **matematiksel muhakeme, matematiksel
problem çözme, matematiksel temsil, veri ile çalışma ve veriye dayalı karar verme, matematiksel araç ve teknoloji
ile çalışma**.

### Years our Learn section covers

The English section covers ages 4/5 to 11 (Reception to Year 6). The Turkish one covers **anasınıfı (the 5-year-old
pre-school year) and 1. to 6. sınıf** (ages 5 to 12): the whole of ilkokul and the first two years of ortaokul.
5. and 6. sınıf are **ortaokul** in Turkey (a new school or building, subject teachers); say so on those pages.

### School calendar and reports

- The year has **two terms ("dönem")** and at least 180 working days (Yönetmelik art. 5). 2026-2027 (press reports of
  the MEB calendar, UNCERTAIN in detail): uyum haftası for anasınıfı and 1. sınıf 7-11 September 2026; lessons start
  **14 September 2026**; **ara tatil** 16-20 November 2026; first term ends 22 January 2027 (report day, "karne
  günü"); **yarıyıl tatili** (two weeks) 25 January to 5 February 2027; second ara tatil 8-12 March 2027; year ends
  **25 June 2027**. On pages say "eylül ortası", "kasım ara tatili", "yarıyıl tatili", "haziran sonu".
- National days children celebrate at school: 29 Ekim Cumhuriyet Bayramı, 10 Kasım (Atatürk anma), 23 Nisan Ulusal
  Egemenlik ve Çocuk Bayramı (the children's festival), 19 Mayıs. Religious holidays (Ramazan Bayramı, Kurban Bayramı)
  move each year; "bayram harçlığı" (holiday pocket money from relatives) is a natural money context.
- **Ilkokul assessment** (Yönetmelik art. 20/2, as amended 2023 and 2025): no marks or tests decide anything;
  progress is followed by observation, activities and game-based assessment. The report shows each subject as
  **"çok iyi", "iyi", "yeterli" or "geliştirilmeli"**. From 2024-2025, starting with 1. sınıf and moving up a year at
  a time, a **"Gelişim Raporu"** replaces the karne in ilkokul ("ayrıca karne verilmez"); until the phase-in reaches a
  grade, that grade still gets a karne. (Which grades have reached it in 2026-2027: UNCERTAIN; most likely 1-3.)
  Note the name clash: Beequation Kids' parents' screen is also called "Gelişim raporu"; on Learn pages, say "okulun
  gelişim raporu" vs "uygulamadaki gelişim raporu" if both appear.
- **Repeating a year in ilkokul** is not the norm ("sınıf tekrarı yaptırılmaması esastır"): only at the parent's
  request after the school informs them in May, and only once in ilkokul (art. 31).
- **Ortaokul assessment**: written exams ("yazılı"), class participation and projects, scored **out of 100** (50 is a
  pass in maths; 70 in Türkçe). A Gelişim Raporu is given with the karne from 5. sınıf (phased from 2025-2026).
  "Teşekkür Belgesi" and "Takdir Belgesi" are certificates for high averages (thresholds not checked: leave out).
- Parents follow attendance and marks on **e-Okul** ("e-Okul Veli Bilgilendirme Sistemi"); "veli toplantısı" is
  the parents' meeting with the teacher.

## 2. What the curriculum expects, year by year

**How the programmes are built.** Each grade's maths is a sequence of **themes**. Ilkokul 1-3: "Sayılar ve
Nicelikler", "İşlemlerden Cebirsel Düşünmeye", "Nesnelerin Geometrisi", "Veriye Dayalı Araştırma"; 4. sınıf (TYMM)
adds probability: "Olayların Olasılığı ve Veriye Dayalı Araştırma". Ortaokul 5-6: "Sayılar ve Nicelikler",
"İşlemlerle Cebirsel Düşünme (ve Değişimler)", "Geometrik Şekiller", "Geometrik Nicelikler", "İstatistiksel Araştırma
Süreci", "Veriden Olasılığa". Measurement, time and money sit inside "Sayılar ve Nicelikler" in ilkokul. The
programme says it leaves out content that isn't meaningful or useful ("daha yalın… bir içerik yapısı"), so some
topics come later than in England (decimals, percentages) and some are gone from these grades (see 10).

Quotes below are from the official texts; "usual practice" items are marked UNCERTAIN.

### Anasınıfı (5–6 yaş; 60-72 ay plans)

Official (TYMM okul öncesi, 60-72 ay, May plan; maths outcomes "MAB"): "1 ile 20 arasında ileriye doğru birer ritmik
sayar"; "10’dan 1’e geriye doğru ritmik sayar"; "1 ile 5 arasında nesne / varlık miktarını saymadan / bir bakışta
söyler" (subitising); "10’a kadar verilen bir sayıdan önce / sonra verilen sayıyı söyler"; "Sayıları küçükten büyüğe /
büyükten küçüğe sıralar"; "1-10 arasındaki miktarları nesne / çizim / rakam gibi farklı temsillerle gösterir";
"Temel geometrik şekilleri isimlendirir"; "Geometrik şekilleri kenar / köşe, sayı ve özelliklerine göre isimlendirir";
"Günün zamanına ilişkin özellikleri söyler"; comparing objects by more than one property; "Gerçekleşmesi mümkün olan /
olmayan durumları söyler"; talking about and comparing solutions to problems. Concepts: "sıra sayısı (birinci,
ikinci…)", "önceki-sonraki", "yarım-tam", "eşit", "az-çok", "sağ-sol".
Usual practice (UNCERTAIN): sorting and patterns ("örüntü"), measuring with hand spans and steps, days of the week,
seasons, simple adding and taking away with objects and fingers. No written sums, no tests.

### 1. sınıf (6–7 yaş), TYMM

- Numbers: digits and numbers **to 20** ("Rakamları ve 20’ye kadar olan sayıları"); counting scattered and ordered
  objects; splitting and joining numbers ("sayıları ayırma ve birleştirme"); 10-20 as "onluk ve birlik" (preparing for
  place value); ordinal numbers; comparing with "çok, daha çok, az, daha az, eşit"; **ritmik sayma** forwards to 100
  in 1s, 5s and 10s, and forwards and backwards within 20 in 1s and 2s ("100’e kadar ileriye doğru birer, beşer ve
  onar; 20’ye kadar ileriye/geriye doğru birer ve ikişer"); growing and shrinking number and shape patterns; estimating up
  to 20 objects; zero.
- Addition and subtraction: everyday "artma / azalma" situations; **adding without carrying ("eldesiz") with sums up to
  20**, written **"alt alta" (in columns) and "yan yana" (in a row)**, two numbers only; subtracting from numbers to 20
  without exchanging ("onluk bozma gerektirmeyen"); the names "toplanan, toplam, artı işareti, eksilen, çıkan, fark,
  eksi işareti"; strategies "büyük sayıya küçük sayı ekleme", **"10’a tamamlama"** (making 10), counting back and
  subtracting in steps; commutativity of addition; the meaning of "=" (balance: "tahterevalli", "eşit kollu terazi");
  the inverse link between + and −; missing numbers; one-step problems.
- Measurement: estimating and measuring length and mass with non-standard units ("standart olmayan ölçme araçları":
  karış, adım, ataş…).
- Money: recognising **1 TL, 5 TL, 10 TL, 20 TL, 50 TL, 100 TL and 200 TL** and ordering them by value (200 TL "only
  shown"); the symbol and abbreviation of the lira (₺, TL); buying in a role-play shop (manav, pazar, kırtasiye).
- Geometry: directions and distances ("yer, yön, konum"; following instructions to a target); matching identical objects;
  sorting objects as "yuvarlak / köşeli"; finding **üçgen, kare, dikdörtgen, çember** in buildings and sorting them by
  corners and sides.
- Data: sorting objects into categories; "çetele" (tally), "sıklık tablosu", "nesne grafiği"; deciding from data.
- Note: **no time-telling outcome in the TYMM 1. sınıf programme** (the clock starts in 2. sınıf).

### 2. sınıf (7–8 yaş), TYMM

- Numbers **to 100**: two-digit numbers, "basamak" and "basamak değeri" (tens and ones); ordering; number line
  ("sayı doğrusu"); **rounding to the nearest ten** ("en yakın onluğa yuvarlama"); ritmik sayma on the "yüzlük tablo"
  (hundred square) in 2s, 3s, 4s, 5s and 10s; patterns; estimating up to 50 objects.
- Fractions (no fraction notation yet): **"bütün, yarım ve çeyrek"** (whole, half, quarter) and how they relate.
- Addition and subtraction to 100: **"eldeli toplama"** (with carrying; two or three numbers, sums not over 100) and
  **"onluk bozarak çıkarma"** (exchanging a ten); problems with up to two steps; "deste" (10) and "düzine" (12) in
  examples.
- Multiplication and division begin: multiplication as **repeated addition and skip counting**, the "×" sign,
  **multiplying numbers to 10 by 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5, building the çarpım tablosu up to 5**; × by 0 and 1;
  commutativity. Division as **repeated subtraction / sharing**, the "÷" sign, **exact division of numbers to 20 by 2,
  3, 4 and 5** ("kalansız"). One-step × and ÷ problems. "=" in all four operations.
- Money: coins and notes; **kuruş as the sub-unit of the lira, TL–kuruş conversions**; "iki tane 25 kuruş = 50 kuruş,
  dört tane 25 kuruş veya iki tane 50 kuruş = 1 TL", linked to half and quarter; a shop with prices in kuruş.
- Time: reading **"tam, yarım ve çeyrek" times** on an analogue clock ("akrep" = hour hand, "yelkovan" = minute hand),
  reading and writing digital time, planning a day ("sabah, öğleden önce, öğle, öğleden sonra, akşam"), the calendar,
  links "dakika-saat, saat-gün, gün-hafta, gün-hafta-ay, ay-mevsim, mevsim-yıl".
- Measurement: standard units **metre, santimetre, kilogram, gram**, estimating and measuring; liquid with
  non-standard containers.
- Geometry: 3D shapes ("geometrik cisimler") in everyday objects and building with them; making models from 2D shapes;
  a shape keeps its properties when turned or resized; routes; spotting **symmetric** shapes.
- Data: up to two categorical data groups; "şekil grafiği" (pictogram) appears in the data notes (UNCERTAIN which
  grade exactly; it is named in the 2. and 3. sınıf data notes).

### 3. sınıf (8–9 yaş), TYMM

- Numbers **to 1000**: reading, writing, place value of three-digit numbers; ordering; ritmik sayma forwards and
  backwards; **"tek / çift"** (odd and even) and whether a sum of odd and even numbers is odd or even; patterns;
  estimating up to 100 objects.
- Fractions: **fraction notation for whole, half, quarter**; **"birim kesir"** (unit fraction) as equal parts of a
  whole; **"pay" and "payda"** (numerator, denominator).
- Addition and subtraction to 1000, with carrying and exchanging, two or three addends (sum under 1000); brackets
  with three addends; estimation and mental methods; problems with up to three steps.
- Multiplication and division: **the çarpım tablosu up to 10 × 10** ("10’a kadar (10 dâhil) çarpım tablosu
  oluşturulur"; numbers to 10 × digits to 9); two-digit × one- and two-digit, up to three-digit × one-digit; **×10 and
  ×100**, ÷10 shortcut; **division of numbers to 100 by a one-digit number, with and without remainder** ("kalanlı ve
  kalansız"); terms **"çarpan, çarpım; bölünen, bölen, bölüm, kalan"**; checking × with ÷ and ÷ with × ("sağlama");
  "kat" (times as many); problems with up to two × or ÷ steps; following instructions of up to 5 steps.
- Time: **analogue and digital clocks to the minute**, **saniye**, conversions second-minute-hour-day-week-month-year,
  "öğleden önce / öğleden sonra" and the 24-hour day; **Roman numerals 1 to 24** ("1’den 24’e kadar Romen
  rakamlarını yazmaları") through clock faces; estimating how long things take.
- Measurement: **mm, cm, m, km; g, kg, ton** and converting within them ("ton ve kilometre hariç"); "10 tane 100
  gramlık = 1 kilogram"; liquids in the standard unit **litre ("litre (L)") and "yarım litre"** (mililitre is not in
  TYMM 3. sınıf; it comes in 4. sınıf); **perimeter ("çevre uzunluğu")** by
  estimating and measuring.
- Money: converting between coins and notes ("Beş tane 1 TL’lik madenî paranın kâğıt 5 TL olduğu"), amounts up to
  200 TL and then beyond.
- Geometry: faces, edges, vertices of 3D shapes ("yüz, ayrıt, köşe"); naming polygons by sides: **üçgen, dörtgen,
  beşgen, altıgen, sekizgen**; drawing shapes with tools; **lines of symmetry** (square, rectangle and "daire" have
  more than one); completing symmetric shapes; coding a symmetric drawing.
- Data: one data group, categorical or counted; çetele, sıklık tablosu, şekil grafiği.

### 4. sınıf (9–10 yaş): 2018 programme in 2026-2027

2018 outcomes (`M.4…`):
- Numbers: **4, 5 and 6-digit numbers**; counting in 100s and 1000s to 10 000; "bölük" (periods) and place value;
  **rounding to the nearest 10 or 100**; ordering with < >; growing/shrinking patterns.
- + and −: up to four digits; estimating; adding multiples of 100 mentally; problems.
- ×: **three-digit × two-digit**; order of factors with three numbers; × by multiples of 10, 100, 1000; **shortcuts
  × 5, 25, 50**; × 10, 100, 1000 mentally; estimating.
- ÷: **three-digit ÷ up to two-digit**; four-digit ÷ one-digit; ÷ 10, 100, 1000 mentally; estimating; × and ÷ as
  inverses; missing values in equalities ("8 + □ = 15 − 3"), the sign "≠".
- Fractions: **"basit, bileşik ve tam sayılı kesir"** (proper, improper, mixed) with models; comparing and ordering
  **unit fractions (denominator up to 20)**; **a fraction of an amount** (no fraction × whole number); comparing up to
  three fractions with the same denominator; **adding and subtracting fractions with the same denominator**.
- Geometry: sides and corners of triangle, square, rectangle; classifying triangles by sides; net of a cube; building
  from isometric drawings; mirror symmetry; **angles**: naming, measuring with non-standard units then with a
  protractor ("açıölçer"), **"dar, dik, geniş, doğru açı"**.
- Measurement: millimetre; converting length units; perimeter of squares and rectangles; **area as the number of
  "birimkare"** (unit squares) and with × for rectangles; **time** conversions hour-minute-second, year-month-week-day
  (with leap years), abbreviations **"saat (sa.), dakika (dk.), saniye (sn.)"**; **mass**: half and quarter kilo in
  grams, ton and **miligram**; **liquids: litre and mililitre**.
- Data: **"sütun grafiği"** (bar chart): reading, making, using with tables.
- Coming (TYMM 4. sınıf, from 2027-2028, UNCERTAIN date): up to six-digit numbers, fractions (proper, improper,
  mixed, **"denk kesirler"**, comparing, fraction of an amount, same-denominator + and −), four-digit + and −, × and ÷
  with shortcuts for 10, 100, 1000 and 5, 25, 50, angle as turning, dar/geniş with the right angle as reference,
  symmetry, area estimated in non-standard units, probability words **"imkânsız, olabilir, kesin"**, up to two data
  groups.

### 5. sınıf (10–11 yaş), TYMM, first year of ortaokul

- Numbers beyond six digits (generalising the reading of big numbers); **problems with all four operations**: + and −
  up to five digits, × at most three-digit × three-digit, ÷ at most four-digit ÷ two-digit.
- **Fractions in different forms: "bileşik, tam sayılı, ondalık, yüzde"** (improper, mixed, decimal, percentage):
  equivalent fractions by "sadeleştirme" and "genişletme"; from fractions with denominator 10 and 100 to decimal and
  percent notation; models "yüzlük kart", number line, "kesir takımları"; comparing fractions in different forms.
  Contexts in the programme: shopping receipts and **KDV** (VAT).
- Algebraic thinking: keeping an equality balanced and properties of operations; **"işlem önceliği"** (order of
  operations); rules of number and shape patterns; **algorithms** in pseudocode, flowcharts and plain language.
- Geometry: basic constructions with tools (pergel, cetvel, gönye); **measuring angles with a protractor**; lines in a
  plane; polygons; circles; constructing triangles.
- "Geometrik Nicelikler": **perimeter and area of a rectangle** (area from unit squares; perimeter from area and back).
- Data: frequency tables, **dot plot ("nokta grafiği") → bar chart ("sütun grafiği")**, **pie chart ("daire grafiği")**;
  discussing misleading graphs. Probability: **the probability scale from 0 ("imkânsız") to 1 ("kesin")**, more or
  less likely.
- Not in 5. sınıf (TYMM): time, money units, metric conversions as separate topics; integers; ratio.

### 6. sınıf (11–12 yaş), TYMM

- **"Çarpanlar ve katlar"**: factors and multiples; **divisibility rules for 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9 and 10**; primes and
  prime factors; **common multiples and common divisors** ("ortak kat, ortak bölen") in problems. **The TYMM
  programme says EBOB and EKOK are not covered** ("en büyük ortak bölen (EBOB) ve en küçük ortak kat (EKOK)
  kavramlarına değinilmez", MAT.6.1.4 notes). The 2018 programme also kept them out of 6. sınıf (M.6.1.2.5: "…(EBOB) ve
  …(EKOK) bulmaya yönelik problemlere bu sınıf düzeyinde girilmez"; they were 8. sınıf, M.8.1.1.2). Parents know the
  names from their own school days, so a page may say in one line that EBOB/EKOK come later, but must not teach them
  or list them as 6. sınıf content.
- **Decimals**: place value of decimal notation through fractions; the link between a fraction and division;
  **problems with all four operations on fractions, decimals and percentages**; standard length units.
- Algebra: **"cebirsel ifadeler"** from real situations (e.g. "3 + k", "d − 4"); patterns; algorithms with algebraic
  expressions.
- Geometry: angles made by two parallel lines and a transversal; quadrilaterals (yamuk, paralelkenar, eşkenar
  dörtgen, dikdörtgen, kare) and their angles; diagonals.
- Measurement: length and **area units**; **area of a parallelogram and a triangle**; **circumference and the
  diameter (π)**; central angle and arc.
- Data: discrete numerical data, **"kök-yaprak gösterimi"** (stem-and-leaf), **"aritmetik ortalama, ortanca, tepe

- Not in 6. sınıf (TYMM): integers ("tam sayılar") and **ratio and proportion ("oran-orantı") are in 7. sınıf**.
- A **national common written exam in maths** ("ülke geneli ortak yazılı sınav") is held in 6. sınıf (see 3).

## 4. Written methods and how they are laid out

Turkish children call written calculation **"işlem"** ("toplama işlemi", "bölme işlemi"); "alt alta toplama"
(column addition), "alt alta yazmak" (writing in columns, units under units: "birler basamağı birler basamağının
altına"). The programme always starts from models ("onluk taban blokları", "basamak tablosu", "sayı doğrusu") and
mental strategies, then the written method. Work starts **from the ones ("birler basamağından başlanır")**.
Checking a result is **"sağlama"** ("işlemin sağlamasını yapmak").

### Eldeli toplama (addition with carrying)

```
    1 1        ← "elde", written small above
    3 4 7
  + 2 8 5
  -------
    6 3 2
```
Said aloud (usual wording, UNCERTAIN in detail): "7 ile 5, 12; 2’yi yazarım, **1 elde**. 4 ile 8, 12, elde 1, 13;
3’ü yazarım, 1 elde. 3 ile 2, 5, elde 1, 6." Terms: "toplanan" (addend), "toplam" (sum), "elde" (carry).
Before carrying, children do "eldesiz toplama" (1. sınıf, sums to 20).

### Onluk bozarak çıkarma (subtraction by exchanging)

Turkey uses decomposition (like England's exchanging): when the top digit is too small, **one ten is "broken" into
ten ones** ("bir onluk bozulur"); children cross out the digit and write the new one above.
```
     4  12  12     ← written small above: hundreds 5 → 4; tens 3 → 2 → 12; ones 2 → 12
     5̶   3̶   2̶
   − 1   7   8
   ----------
     3   5   4
```
"2’den 8 çıkmaz, onlar basamağından bir onluk bozarım: 12’den 8 çıkarsa 4. Onlar basamağında 2 kaldı; 2’den 7 çıkmaz,
yüzler basamağından bir yüzlük bozarım: 12’den 7 çıkarsa 5. Yüzler basamağında 4 kaldı; 4’ten 1 çıkarsa 3." Result
354 (sağlama: 354 + 178 = 532). Terms: **"eksilen"** (minuend), **"çıkan"** (subtrahend), **"fark"** (difference).
Exact way the crossings are drawn varies by teacher; keep pictures simple. Don't show "equal additions" (not used).

### Multiplication ("alt alta çarpma")

```
      2 3 4
    ×   2 3
    -------
      7 0 2      ← 234 × 3
  + 4 6 8        ← 234 × 2 (tens): written one place to the left
    -------
    5 3 8 2
```
Terms: **"çarpan"** (each factor), **"çarpım"** (product), "elde" again. Whether a 0 is written in the empty place:
UNCERTAIN (both seen); don't make an issue of it. Times tables are said "üç kere dört on iki" or "üç çarpı dört
eşittir on iki" (the app reads "çarpı"), written 3 × 4 = 12.

### Division ("bölme işlemi")

The **dividend on the left, the divisor on the right of a vertical line, a horizontal line under the divisor, the
quotient under that line**; the **subtractions are written out in full** under the dividend, each with a minus sign
and a line. This layout is the standard one in Turkish schools (the programme names the parts but doesn't draw it;
layout from usual practice, high confidence, details UNCERTAIN):
```
  8 5 7 | 4
− 8     |-------
  ---   | 2 1 4
  0 5
  − 4
  ---
    1 7
  − 1 6
  -----
      1
```
"8’in içinde 4, 2 kere var; 2 kere 4, 8; 8’den 8 çıkarsa 0. 5’i aşağı indiririm: 5’in içinde 4, 1 kere var; 1 kere 4,
4; 5’ten 4 çıkarsa 1. 7’yi indiririm: 17’nin içinde 4, 4 kere var; 4 kere 4, 16; 17’den 16 çıkarsa 1." Bölüm 214,
kalan 1. Terms: **"bölünen"** (dividend), **"bölen"** (divisor), **"bölüm"** (quotient), **"kalan"** (remainder);
"kalanlı / kalansız bölme". Check: **bölünen = bölen × bölüm + kalan** (4 × 214 + 1 = 857), and the remainder is
always smaller than the divisor ("bölen kalandan daima büyük"). England's "bus stop", the grid method and chunking
are not used; don't show them. Careful: the Turkish word "bölüm" is the quotient, not "a division".

### Shortcuts the programme names

× 10, 100, 1000 ("sonuna sıfır ekleme" as an observed pattern), ÷ 10, 100, 1000 for numbers ending in zeros, ×5
(×10 then halve), ×25, ×50 (2018, 4. sınıf; TYMM 4. sınıf too).

## 5. Notation

- **Decimal comma**: 2,5. In school read **"iki tam onda beş"** (0,25 "sıfır tam yüzde yirmi beş"); everyday "iki
  virgül beş". Never a point for decimals.
- **Thousands**: the curriculum texts write a **space**: "10 000", "45 485", "3 000" (four-digit numbers usually with
  no separator: "1000", "9999"). Prices and everyday print often use a **point**: "1.250 TL" (UNCERTAIN how
  textbooks write prices). Rule for our pages: no separator in four-digit numbers, a non-breaking space from five
  digits (10 000, 250 000, 1 000 000); never in years. Large numbers: "milyon" 10⁶, **"milyar" 10⁹** (= English
  billion; no trap), "trilyon" 10¹².
- **Multiplication**: "×" in primary (read "çarpı", or "kere" in tables). In algebra (6.) the sign may be left out
  ("3k") or written "·" (UNCERTAIN). Don't use "*". The 2018 text sometimes types "x"; we use ×.
- **Division**: **"÷"** is the primary-school sign (2018: "bölme işleminin işaretini (÷) kullanır"; TYMM lists × and ÷),
  read "bölü". ":" also appears in some texts ("12 : 4") and is the sign for **ratio** ("oran", 3:5) in later grades.
  Recommended: ÷ everywhere on our pages.
- **Minus**: "−", read "eksi". **Equals**: "=", read "eşittir" (2 + 3 = 5 "iki artı üç eşittir beş"). **Comparison**:
  "<" "küçüktür", ">" "büyüktür", "≠" "eşit değildir" (2018, 4. sınıf).
- **Fractions**: stacked ¾ (fraction characters fine). Read **denominator first with the locative**: ½ "ikide bir",
  ⅓ "üçte bir", ¾ "dörtte üç", ⅕ "beşte bir", ⅛ "sekizde bir", 7/10 "onda yedi"; everyday "yarım" (half) and
  "çeyrek" (quarter). Mixed numbers: "2 tam 1/2" read "iki tam ikide bir" ("tam sayılı kesir"). The app's
  read-aloud says "3 bölü 4"; pages should use "dörtte üç" and may add that it is also read "3 bölü 4".
- **Percent**: the sign goes **before** the number, no space: **"%25"**, read "yüzde yirmi beş". Important: never
  "25 %" or "25%".
- **Ordinals**: a full stop after the numeral: "1. sınıf", "3. basamak", "23 Nisan". Read "birinci, ikinci…".
- **Suffixes after numerals**: an apostrophe (’) and the suffix follow the **spoken** number's vowel harmony. Get these
  right; they are the most common error in machine-like Turkish:

  | number | dative (-e/-a: "…’e kadar", "…’e çeyrek var") | accusative (-i: "…’i çeyrek geçiyor") | locative (-de: "…’de") |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 bir | 1’e | 1’i | 1’de |
  | 2 iki | 2’ye | 2’yi | 2’de |
  | 3 üç | 3’e | 3’ü | 3’te |
  | 4 dört | 4’e | 4’ü | 4’te |
  | 5 beş | 5’e | 5’i | 5’te |
  | 6 altı | 6’ya | 6’yı | 6’da |
  | 7 yedi | 7’ye | 7’yi | 7’de |
  | 8 sekiz | 8’e | 8’i | 8’de |
  | 9 dokuz | 9’a | 9’u | 9’da |
  | 10 on | 10’a | 10’u | 10’da |
  | 11 on bir | 11’e | 11’i | 11’de |
  | 12 on iki | 12’ye | 12’yi | 12’de |
  | 20 yirmi | 20’ye | 20’yi | 20’de |
  | 30 otuz / 40 kırk | 30’a / 40’a | 30’u / 40’ı | 30’da / 40’ta |
  | 50 elli / 60 altmış | 50’ye / 60’a | 50’yi / 60’ı | 50’de / 60’ta |
  | 70 yetmiş / 80 seksen / 90 doksan | 70’e / 80’e / 90’a | 70’i / 80’i / 90’ı | 70’te / 80’de / 90’da |
  | 100 yüz / 1000 bin | 100’e / 1000’e | 100’ü / 1000’i | 100’de / 1000’de |

  Also on names: "Indie’nin", "Indie’ye", "Ayşe’nin". A word after a number stays singular: "3 elma", "5 çalışma
  kâğıdı".
- **Units**: metric only. Symbols: mm, cm, m, km; g, kg, t, mg; **L and mL** (capital L: the TYMM programme writes
  "litre (L)", the 2018 programme "mililitre (mL)"); time "sa.", "dk.", "sn." (2018) or written in full.
  Measures in recipes use "su bardağı", "çay bardağı", "yemek kaşığı", "çay kaşığı": a good non-standard-unit context.
- **Money**: "12,50 TL" (amount, space, TL) in prose; the symbol "₺" (₺12,50 or 12,50 ₺) is also common on price tags
  and in the icon glyph. "50 kuruş" or "0,50 TL". Read "on iki lira elli kuruş".
- **Times**: TDK puts a **point** between hours and minutes: **"15.30"**, "saat 09.15’te"; digital displays and
  timetables show "15:30". Use "15.30" in sentences and "15:30" only inside a drawn digital clock (UNCERTAIN which
  textbooks prefer).
- **Dates**: day.month.year: 10.10.2026, "10 Ekim 2026". The week starts on **Monday** ("pazartesi").

## 9. Form of address and tone

- **Parents and teachers: "siz"** (formal plural), as in the site and the apps' grown-up screens ("Diğer iki sayının
  toplamı olan sayıya dokunun", "Tarayıcınızda ücretsiz oynayın", "Çocuğunuz…", "Sınıfınız için…"). Imperatives in
  -(y)in/-ın/-un/-ün: "deneyin", "sorun", "bakın". Never "sen" to a parent.
- **Children: "sen"**, as in Beequation Kids ("Sen yaparsın!", "Bir büyüğüne sor"). Use it only where a line clearly
  speaks to the child (a challenge read aloud, Indie talking to a child); otherwise write challenges as plain word
  problems about Indie.
- Parents are "**veliler**" in school contexts ("Veliler için"), "aileler" when warm and general, "anne babalar"
  informally; the app uses "ebeveyn". "Çocuğunuz" in each section, then "çocuklar". Teachers: "öğretmenler",
  "öğrencileriniz", "sınıfınız".
- Tone: warm, plain, direct, like a sınıf öğretmeni talking to a parent at pick-up time. No "çok basit", "kolayca",
  "sadece şunu yapın" in a dismissive way; no "Sayın veli" formality. No emoji. Use the words children hear in class,
  then explain ("sınıfta buna ‘onluk bozma’ deniyor…").
- Typography: Turkish letters always (ç ğ ı İ ö ş ü; capital of i is İ, of ı is I); circumflex where TDK keeps it:
  "kâğıt", "madenî", "imkânsız", "dâhil", "hâlâ". Typographic apostrophe ’ before suffixes. Quotes “…” (the site
  uses curly quotes). Turkish capitalises after a colon only for a full sentence (the site writes "Blitz: Çözebildiğiniz
  kadar çözün").
- **Glossary letters** (site.json `alphabet`): the 29 Turkish letters in order, a b c ç d e f g ğ h ı İ j k l m n o ö p
  r s ş t u ü v y z. The letter i is stored as the capital **"İ"** on purpose: the header menu shows each letter with
  Python's `upper()`, which turns a lowercase "i" into a dotless "I" (the same as ı's capital). So in
  glossary/index.html the i section's heading is `<h2 id="İ">İ</h2>`; every other letter keeps a lowercase id
  (`id="a"`, `id="ç"`, `id="ı"`, `id="ş"`…). Words starting with ı are rare; leave ğ (no Turkish word starts with it)
  and ı, j without entries if nothing fits.
- Indie: Turkish has no gendered pronoun ("o"), so she/her needs no handling; call her "**Arı Indie**", "Indie",
  "minik arımız". Pronounced like "İndi": suffixes "Indie’nin, Indie’ye, Indie’yi, Indie’den".
